# App Workshop productivity app surface catalog

> Corrections from [`06_factcheck.md`](06_factcheck.md) are applied inline and marked **[FC]**.


## Decision

**INFERENCE — A top-tier Apple productivity app ships a dependable core job, quick ways in and out of that job, recoverable data, accessible platform-native interfaces, clear purchase and support journeys, and a release and operating loop.** The full catalog below describes possible surfaces; it does not mean every app should enable every capability.

**INFERENCE — Treat “table stakes” as the minimum for the app’s stated job and supported platforms.** Once the product promises iPhone and iPad, Mac, Watch, or system integrations, the relevant surfaces in those contexts become table stakes. StandBy, collaboration, advanced AI, referrals, and growth experimentation remain differentiators or conditional capabilities unless the product’s job depends on them.

**FACT — RevenueCat documents offerings, entitlements, remote paywalls, experiments, customer lookup, and a self-service Customer Center.** **INFERENCE —** Given the owner’s decision, use RevenueCat as the purchase and subscription operations default, while keeping app data, account identity, and the creator console under separately defined contracts. RevenueCat is not itself the app’s complete customer-support, analytics, release, or product-operations system. [RevenueCat docs](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs) and [Paywalls](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/tools/paywalls) (checked 2026-10-19).

## Labels and reading guide

**FACT** marks documented product capabilities, APIs, or rules. **INFERENCE** marks the table-stakes/differentiator/skip classification and the proposed acceptance check. **UNKNOWN** marks an app-specific decision that cannot be settled without its product, data, and platform choices. There are no third-party market-size or adoption estimates in this catalog.

The example apps show that a surface exists in a real product; examples do not mean those apps implement it identically or that every app needs it. Feature examples are supported by the linked first-party pages: [Things support and feature list](https://culturedcode.com/things/support/articles/1059358/), [Things App Store page](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/things-3/id904237743?platform=watch), [Fantastical help](https://flexibits.com/fantastical-ios/help), [Fantastical feature page](https://flexibits.com/fantastical), [Todoist Siri guide](https://www.todoist.com/help/todoist/features/use-siri-with-todoist-zDdrA4), [Notion offline guide](https://www.notion.com/help/use-pages-offline), and [Goodnotes widgets guide](https://support.goodnotes.com/hc/en-us/articles/15492667891471-Use-Goodnotes-widgets-on-iPhone-iPad-and-Mac) (checked 2026-10-19).

Apple rule references use the current [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (checked 2026-10-19). Acceptance checks below are proposed minimums, not Apple certification or a guarantee of App Review approval.

## USER — the person using the app

| **First run and first useful result** | **FACT:** Onboarding guidance recommends a fast, optional flow, useful defaults, and delaying nonessential setup. **INFERENCE:** Make the first session complete the app’s main job, not merely configure the app. Examples: Things, Todoist, Fantastical. [Apple Onboarding HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding) | SwiftUI flow and persisted progress; use TipKit for contextual tips where it fits. | **INFERENCE — Table stakes.** | **FACT:** Review §2.1 requires a complete, reviewable app; §2.3 requires accurate metadata. Do not hide core functionality behind an unexplained account or setup gate. [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) | **INFERENCE:** Fresh install → reach the main screen → create and revisit one useful item without account creation unless the core job requires it. |
| **Permission priming and denial recovery** | **FACT:** Explain the reason in context and request access when the feature needs it; offer an alternative where practical. Examples: Fantastical explains calendar, contacts, and location use during relevant setup. [Privacy HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy), [Fantastical setup](https://flexibits.com/fantastical-ios/help/getting-started) | Native permission prompts; EventKit, UserNotifications, Photos, Contacts, etc. only for selected capabilities. | **INFERENCE — Table stakes for any protected resource used.** | **FACT:** §§5.1.1 and 5.1 require appropriate consent and privacy practices. [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) | **INFERENCE:** Deny each selected permission; verify the core job still works where possible, the feature explains the limit, and Settings recovery is clear. |
| **Core list, detail, editor, and empty/error states** | **INFERENCE:** This is the product itself: create, read, edit, complete/archive, and recover from an invalid or empty state. [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [App Intents](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appintents/getting-started-with-the-app-intents-framework) | **INFERENCE:** Run capture and lookup from Shortcuts and Siri with the app closed; verify parameters, result, cancellation, and duplicate prevention. |
| **Search inside the app and Spotlight indexing** | **FACT:** App Intents and Core Spotlight support exposing searchable app content; Things and Goodnotes document in-app search. [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) | **INFERENCE:** Search an item in-app and Spotlight; tap the Spotlight result to open the correct record; delete it and confirm the index no longer returns it. |

[Core Data](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coredata/), [CloudKit sync](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coredata/syncing-a-core-data-store-with-cloudkit) | Native Apple-only path: SwiftData/Core Data + CloudKit. ## CUSTOMER — the person paying or needing help

| Surface | What it is; examples | Implementation | Class | App Review gotcha | Minimal acceptance test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Paywall, trial, and entitlement** | **FACT:** RevenueCat supports offerings, packages, entitlements, and remotely configured paywalls. App Review requires clear ongoing subscription value and accurate purchase terms. [RevenueCat Paywalls](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/tools/paywalls), [Guidelines §3.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) | RevenueCat Purchases SDK + Paywalls; entitlements gate paid features, not user-owned data. | **INFERENCE — Table stakes if the product has paid access; otherwise skip-unless monetization is chosen.** | **FACT:** §3.1.2 requires clear price, period, trial conversion, and terms; §3.1.1 generally requires IAP for digital features. [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) | **INFERENCE:** Test eligible/ineligible trial, purchase success/failure, restore, renewal/expiry, offline state, and exact rendered price/disclosures. |
| **Win-back, offer codes, promotional offers** | **FACT:** RevenueCat documents introductory, promotional, offer-code, and win-back offers; current docs identify OS/SDK and configuration requirements. [RevenueCat offers](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/subscription-guidance/subscription-offers/ios-subscription-offers), [paywall offers](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/tools/paywalls/creating-paywalls/supporting-offers) | RevenueCat + App Store Connect offer configuration; eligibility determined by Apple/offer setup. | **INFERENCE — Differentiator; skip-unless there is a lapsed-customer or offer-management job.** | **FACT:** §3.1.2: offer terms and actual eligibility must be truthful; do not display an ineligible offer as guaranteed. ## Ranked: 25 highest-value boilerplate surfaces

**INFERENCE — This ranking favors a first app’s user trust, core utility, and ability to ship and operate.** It assumes the owner’s RevenueCat-first direction and selected iPhone+iPad, Mac, Watch, widgets, Live Activities, and App Intents scope. A generated app should still select capabilities by product need.

1. **Core list/detail/editor and reliable save states** — the product’s actual job.
2. **First-run path to a useful result** — fast start with sensible defaults.
3. **Local persistence and offline-safe editing** — no lost user work.
4. **Accessible controls, Dynamic Type, VoiceOver, and reduced motion** — quality baseline across screens.
5. **Permission timing, explanation, and denial recovery** — needed only for selected protected resources.
6. **Search inside the app** — essential once users accumulate content.
7. **Import/export and data portability** — user control over durable content.
8. **Undo for destructive or consequential editing** — recovery without support.
9. **App Intents for the core capture and lookup actions** — common system integration foundation.
10. **Shortcuts/Siri actions with useful parameters and results** — Apple workflow integration.
11. **Spotlight indexing and correct deep-link routing** — system discovery into the right record.
12. **Share extension or equivalent quick capture** — for products that collect from other apps.
13. **Reminders/notifications with consent and preference controls** — for time-sensitive workflows.
14. **iPad and Mac native keyboard, menus, focus, and window behavior** — required by selected platforms.
15. **Cross-device sync contract and conflict recovery** — CloudKit by default for Apple-only sync; backend only for a named need.
16. **Widgets for selected high-value glance/action cases** — Home and Lock Screen surfaces.
17. **Watch capture/view/complete flow** — because Watch is in the selected support promise.
18. **WidgetKit Controls / Action button where the core action is one tap** — use only when useful.
19. **RevenueCat paywall, entitlement, and purchase path** — when the product has paid value.
20. **Restore, manage/cancel, billing help, and refund path** — complete customer lifecycle for paid apps.
21. **Account deletion and export path when accounts exist** — with billing and retention communication.
22. **Privacy manifest, SDK inventory, and App Store privacy answers** — checked for each release and dependency change.
23. **Support/feedback route and App Store review operations** — close the loop with customers.
24. **TestFlight, release notes, crash/diagnostic visibility, and a fix-forward plan** — required to learn and operate safely.
25. **Creator view of revenue, entitlements, crashes, and support context** — begin with RevenueCat and App Store Connect; build the custom console after the operator workflow is concrete.

**INFERENCE — Defer until a product case is clear:** collaboration, long-term version history, broad theme/icon packs, Focus filters, StandBy, Watch complications, Live Activities, Foundation Models, Visual Intelligence, win-back experiments, A/B testing, referrals, global hotkeys, and a forced-update system. They can be excellent differentiators, but they add product, privacy, support, or operational work.

**UNKNOWN — The catalog cannot settle:** final minimum OS versions; which app data is sensitive; whether collaboration or accounts are necessary; required backend and retention policy; price/trial/Family Sharing choices; analytics vendor; legal obligations by market; or which creator-console operations should be self-service versus owner-only. These need the selected app brief and a fresh implementation-time review.

**Not checked:** I did not audit the generator, inspect every named app, or validate these APIs against a pinned SDK/device matrix. The principal risk is treating a catalog acceptance test as release evidence; each selected surface still needs current SDK, privacy, hardware, and App Review verification. The repository’s prior App Workshop review was a separate, older snapshot and was not treated as current implementation evidence.
