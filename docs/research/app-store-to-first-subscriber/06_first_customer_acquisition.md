# Getting a First Paying, Then First Renewing, Subscriber on the App Store (2026)

Status: COMPLETE. Scope: iOS/App Store demand side for a solo developer shipping a new subscription app. Evidence graded FACT (primary source, URL) / INFERENCE (reasoned from FACTs) / ANECDOTE (single-operator report, not generalizable) / UNKNOWN (searched, not found or paywalled). Steps tagged HUMAN-ONLY / AGENT-CAN-DO / AGENT-CAN-OBSERVE where relevant. All benchmark reports are vendor-platform samples (RevenueCat, Adapty), not random samples of all App Store apps — apps that adopt a metering/paywall SDK skew toward more sophisticated, already-monetizing operators. Treat every percentage below as a hypothesis to test against your own cohort, not a target to hit.

## 1. Benchmarks (RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps, Adapty, Appfigures, Sensor Tower, Business of Apps)

### Takeaway
Two large vendor panels (RevenueCat: 115,000+ apps/$16B; Adapty: 16,000 apps/$3B, both reporting in 2026) agree on the broad shape: hard paywalls convert far better than freemium on paper (roughly 5–8x on D35 conversion and revenue-per-install), annual-subscription churn is severe in month 1 (30–35% of all yearly cancellations), and app revenue is brutally concentrated — the top 10% of apps take ~94.5% of all subscription revenue, and roughly 58–81% of new subscription apps never clear $1,000–$10,000 in total/monthly revenue. Both panels are self-selected (apps that installed a metering SDK) and neither discusses this selection bias explicitly, so treat medians as "what sophisticated operators achieve," not population averages, and treat the 2025→2026 year-over-year comparisons as directionally interesting rather than precise.

### Cited Findings
- RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026: sample of 115,000+ subscription apps, $16B+ revenue tracked, published March 19, 2026 (updated April 22, 2026). No explicit selection-bias discussion. — [RevenueCat blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)
- Hard paywall median D35 download-to-paid conversion: 10.7% (down from 12.1% in the 2025 report) vs. freemium 2.1% — roughly 5x. — [RevenueCat blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)
- Hard paywall Day-60 revenue-per-install (RPI): $3.09 vs. freemium $0.38 — roughly 8x. One-year retention is statistically similar (hard 27% vs. freemium 28%), so the paywall-model gap is almost entirely an acquisition/conversion effect, not a retention effect. — [RevenueCat blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)
- Trial length: 17–32 day trials convert at 42.5% vs. <4-day trials at 25.5% (~70% better), yet 46.5% of apps in 2026 use trials under 4 days (up from 42.1% in 2025) — most operators are moving the opposite direction of what raw conversion data favors, likely trading conversion rate for faster cash/decision cycles. — [RevenueCat blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)
- Day-0 cancellation: 55.4% of all 3-day-trial cancellations happen on day 0 itself (up from ~51% in 2025); 84% happen within day 0–1. — [RevenueCat blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)
- Annual-subscription churn: month 1 accounts for 35% of all annual cancellations; full year-1 churn is ~72% (worsened from ~56% in 2025). — [RevenueCat blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)
- Revenue concentration/polarization: top-quartile apps grew MRR 80%+ year-over-year while bottom-quartile apps declined >33% — a 113-point spread; in the 2025 edition, the top 5% of newly launched apps earned 400x more in year 1 than the bottom 25% (up from 200x in 2024). — [RevenueCat blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026); [Subscription Insider summary of 2025 report](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-report-ais-dominance-retention-challenges-and-the-shift-away-from-pure-subscriptions)
- Adapty State of In-App Subscriptions 2026: sample of 16,000 apps, $3B revenue tracked, 105,000 paywalls compared, published March 20, 2026 (article dates suggest it substantially reflects 2025 transaction data). No explicit sampling-methodology or refund-rate disclosure. — [Adapty report](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/)
- Global averages: install-to-trial 10.9%, trial-to-paid 25.6% (category range: Health & Fitness ~35% trial-to-paid down to Entertainment ~19%). 90% of trial starts happen on day 0 of install. — [Adapty report](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/)
- Revenue distribution (Adapty, new subscription apps): 57.7–59.3% never exceed $1,000 in *total* lifetime revenue; 22.4% earn $1,001–$10,000; 12% earn $10,001–$100,000; only 7.2–7.9% cross $100,000. Top 10% of apps capture 94.5% of all subscription revenue (up from 92.7% in 2023). — [Adapty revenue concentration post](https://adapty.io/blog/app-subscription-revenue-concentration/)
- Business of Apps (aggregating other sources, updated 2026): true median monthly app revenue fell 22% year-over-year from $627 to $492; one year after launch the median app makes ~$72/month, top-quartile apps $429/month, top 10% $2,574/month; ~81% of apps never reach $1,000/month within their first two years, and only 4.6% reach $10K MRR. — [Business of Apps app revenue data](https://www.businessofapps.com/data/app-revenues/) (secondary-aggregator search summary; original methodology not independently verified in this pass)
- Renewal rates by category and plan duration (RevenueCat 2026, same 115,000-app panel): annual medians range from 23% (Productivity) to 40% (Travel/Business); monthly medians range from 42% (Social & Lifestyle) to 61% (Business); weekly medians range from 35% (Social & Lifestyle) to 58% (Education). Business apps are stickiest across all durations. — [RevenueCat renewal-rates-by-category](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/average-subscription-renewal-rates-by-app-category)
- Appfigures: no independently sourced trial/conversion benchmark article was located in this pass (search results surfaced only product/pricing pages, not a benchmark report comparable to RevenueCat/Adapty). — UNKNOWN, see Gaps.
- Sensor Tower: not directly queried in this pass due to tool-budget constraints; no claim sourced to Sensor Tower in this note. — UNKNOWN, see Gaps.

### Inferences
- Because hard-paywall and freemium apps show near-identical 1-year retention (27% vs 28%) despite an 8x RPI gap, the practical lesson for a solo developer is that paywall *placement/hardness* mainly changes who pays and how much, not how well you retain who pays — pricing/paywall strategy and retention strategy are separable problems.
- The RevenueCat report explicitly frames 2026 as a year where "good enough" business models are being eliminated (top/bottom spread widening from ~200x to ~400x, then to a 113-point MRR-growth gap) — this reads as survivorship-selection language: as the metering-SDK panel matures, apps that quietly failed are more likely to have already stopped reporting, which would mechanically make the surviving cohort look more polarized over time even without any real change in market difficulty. Treat the "market is getting harder" narrative with additional skepticism due to this dynamic.
- The gap between RevenueCat's per-category renewal-rate table and Adapty's per-plan retention numbers (e.g., Adapty's weekly-trial Day-380 retention of 5.5% vs. RevenueCat's weekly first-renewal rates of 35–58%) is not a contradiction — they measure different things (single first-renewal survival vs. cumulative 380-day survival) — but a reader skimming headlines could easily conflate them. Any downstream doc should specify which metric (first-renewal vs. cumulative long-horizon retention) is being cited.

### Gaps
- No RevenueCat- or Adapty-scale benchmark report attributable specifically to Appfigures or Sensor Tower was retrieved in this pass; both would need a dedicated fetch of their respective blogs/report pages (appfigures.com/blog, sensortower.com/blog) which the tool-quota interruption prevented reaching directly (only reached via secondary Google-search snippets, insufficiently detailed to cite as primary).
- Neither RevenueCat nor Adapty publishes an explicit refund-rate benchmark in the pages reached in this pass (Adapty's own summary flags "no explicit discussion of ... refund rates").
- Business of Apps' underlying methodology (is it a straight aggregation of other named sources, or its own panel?) was not independently confirmed; treat its numbers as a secondary aggregator, one step removed from raw data.

## 2. The funnel to a first renewal and its duration

### Takeaway
The realistic funnel is: impression → product-page view → download → onboarding → paywall view → trial-start (or direct purchase) → trial-to-paid conversion → first renewal, and the entire trial-start decision is overwhelmingly a day-0 event (~80–90% of trial starts happen on day 0 across both major reports), so the "time to first renewal" is essentially fixed by plan length: about 3–7 days after install for a weekly plan's second cycle, ~1 month for monthly, ~12 months for annual (all starting the trial clock on day 0). First renewal is also where the steepest subscriber loss happens — worse than any later renewal.

### Cited Findings
- RevenueCat's "essential guide to mobile paywalls" (Dec 2024, refreshed dataset context): only 1.7% of app downloads convert to a paying subscriber within the first 30 days on average; top-performing apps achieve ~4.2%. This is the download→paid conversion for the *whole* funnel, not trial-to-paid alone. — [RevenueCat mobile paywalls guide, via Google snippet](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/) (exact URL for this specific guide could not be reloaded after a 404 on a guessed slug; cite with caution — see Gaps)
- 80–90% of trial starts occur on day 0 of install (RevenueCat: 80%; Adapty: 90%). Adapty additionally reports 44.5% of *all purchases* (not just trial starts) happen on day 0. — [RevenueCat 2026 report](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026); [Adapty high-performing-paywall analysis](https://adapty.io/blog/high-performing-paywall-2026/)
- First-renewal churn is the single worst point in the subscription lifecycle: weekly plans lose 42–65% of subscribers at first renewal, monthly plans lose 39–58%, annual plans lose 60–77% (i.e., only 23–40% return for year two). Once a subscriber survives to a second renewal, retention improves sharply — e.g., weekly subscribers who reach a second renewal show 74–91% retention into the third cycle (a 25–30 point jump). — [RevenueCat "curse of the first renewal"](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/first-renewal-churn)
- Activation windows before the renewal decision firms up: weekly plans, the first 5 days; monthly plans, the first 2 weeks; annual plans, the first month — matching the finding that 23–50% of annual cancellations happen in month 1 (another 8–10% in month 2). — [RevenueCat "curse of the first renewal"](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/first-renewal-churn)
- Trial-linked retention: weekly subscribers with a trial go from 23% to 42% Day-30 retention compared with weekly subscribers without a trial — trials materially help short-plan retention even though they don't necessarily lift raw conversion. — [Adapty via search summary](https://adapty.io/blog/trial-conversion-rates-for-in-app-subscriptions/)
- Adapty Day-380 (roughly year-1) retention by plan-with-trial: annual 19.9%, monthly 14.2%, weekly 5.5%; trial subscribers overall retain 1.4–1.7x better than users who bought directly without a trial. — [Adapty report](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/)
- RevenueCat's RENEWAL server-notification/webhook event (not literally named "DID_RENEW"; that name is the StoreKit 2 / App Store Server Notifications V2 equivalent) fires "when an existing subscription was renewed, or a lapsed user resubscribed" and is generated once the renewal payment is confirmed successfully — this is the concrete, observable proxy for "first renewal" a solo developer should instrument. — [RevenueCat event types docs](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/integrations/webhooks/event-types-and-fields)

### Inferences
- Because ~80–90% of trial starts and ~44.5% of purchases happen on day 0, a solo developer's entire "conversion" lever set (onboarding quality, paywall copy, paywall timing) is effectively a single-shot decision made in one session — there is little practical "come back later and convert" funnel to optimize for a new, low-traffic app. This means iteration speed on the onboarding→paywall sequence (via App Store Connect Product Page Optimization or a remote-config paywall tool) matters more than post-install email/push re-engagement campaigns for a pre-PMF solo app.
- Time-to-first-renewal-observation, concretely: weekly plan ≈ 7–10 days from install (trial + one billing cycle); monthly plan with a 7-day trial ≈ 30–37 days; annual plan with a 7-day trial ≈ ~372 days. A solo developer choosing weekly billing will have a real renewal signal (and thus a real read on product-market fit) in under two weeks; choosing annual-only defers that signal for a year — a major reason many indie operators default to weekly or monthly for a new app despite weekly's worse long-run retention.

### Gaps
- The specific RevenueCat article URL for the "1.7% / 4.2%" download-to-paid statistic returned a 404 when re-fetched directly; the figure is included above via the original Google-search-result summary quote and should be re-verified against a live RevenueCat blog search before being treated as settled fact.
- No source in this pass gave an explicit "median calendar days from app-launch-day to an app's first-ever renewal event" for a *brand-new small app* specifically (as opposed to category-wide medians) — this would require either an indie postmortem with dated numbers or a RevenueCat/Adapty cohort cut not found in this pass.

## 3. Reasons a launched app gets zero subscribers; realistic targets/timelines for 1, 10, 100 subscribers

### Takeaway
Evidence here is almost entirely ANECDOTE-tier (individual builder blog posts and forum threads), not benchmarked studies — there is no rigorous, sourced study of "why do apps get zero subscribers" analogous to the RevenueCat/Adapty panels. The recurring anecdotal causes are: no distribution/awareness plan (build-then-hope), no evidence of demand before building, and treating downloads as the goal instead of downloads-to-paid; realistic first-milestone timelines reported by named individuals range from days (via an existing audience or a Product Hunt spike) to months of stagnation at zero.

### Cited Findings
- A tracked, dated "Indie Day 60" postmortem reports reaching day 60 of a build-in-public journey with zero paying customers and $0 MRR, explicitly reframing the milestone as "fine" and redirecting attention to leading indicators (audience size, article views) rather than revenue — an explicit counter-example to survivorship-biased success stories. — ANECDOTE — [dev.to, Indie Day 60](https://dev.to/snake_sun/indie-day-60-i-have-zero-paying-customers-and-it-is-fine-2g08)
- A separate Indie Hackers post ("2 months in, 700 downloads, and I'm stuck") documents an app that generated real download volume but stalled short of meaningful paid conversion, illustrating that downloads alone are not evidence the acquisition problem is solved. — ANECDOTE — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/the-indie-makers-dilemma-2-months-in-700-downloads-and-i-m-stuck-53db8107c1)
- Adapty's own "how to get your first subscribers" interview-style piece with an indie developer explicitly targets this exact problem (zero-to-first-subscriber) as a common, named pain point worth a dedicated article — its existence is itself evidence the problem is common enough to be a recognized genre, though the piece is promotional/vendor content rather than a study. — ANECDOTE/vendor content — [Adapty blog](https://adapty.io/blog/how-to-get-your-first-subscribers/)
- Indie Hackers community threads on "how/where did you find your first 1, 10, 100 customers" report highly variable timelines: one founder's first customer arrived in 3 days from a personal network; another got 14 sign-ups from a single subreddit post; Product Hunt launches are repeatedly credited with jumps to 100+ users "in a few hours" or "in a few days." These are self-selected success reports (people who post such threads tend to be the ones who succeeded), so should not be read as typical. — ANECDOTE — [Indie Hackers thread](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-where-did-you-find-your-first-1-10-and-100-customer-f146e8e3d3)

### Inferences
- The strongest pattern across ANECDOTE sources is that "first customer" timing is dominated by whether the developer already had *some* audience or channel (personal network, an existing newsletter/following, a subreddit where they're a known contributor) before launch — cold, channel-less launches show materially longer or indefinite times to first dollar. This is consistent with (though not proven by) the launch-channel evidence in Section 4.
- Because no rigorous denominator exists (nobody tracks how many indie apps *never* get a subscriber and stay silent), any specific probability or percentage claim about "X% of apps get zero subscribers" would be fabricated; this section intentionally reports no such number. Any generated-app template guidance should say "budget for a real chance of zero" rather than "expect N% odds."

### Gaps
- No study quantifies the population-level rate of apps that launch and get zero subscribers (survivorship bias in every available source: developers who fail silently rarely publish a postmortem, and business-of-apps-style panels only include apps active enough to be tracked).
- No sourced, dated case study specifically ties a *documented* zero-to-one moment to a specific ASO or paywall change for a solo-built subscription iOS app (as opposed to SaaS/web indie-hacker examples, which dominate the Indie Hackers corpus and are not App Store-specific — see caveat in Section 4).

## 4. Organic launch channels credible solo developers report working

### Takeaway
The credible organic channels for a solo App Store launch — TestFlight/pre-launch waitlists, Product Hunt, niche community posts (Reddit/Discord/Slack), build-in-public on X, short-form video, and newsletters — all have real anecdotal support, but almost none of it is App Store-subscription-specific: most of the concrete numbers ("first 100 customers in a few hours," "14 sign-ups from one subreddit post") come from web/SaaS Indie Hackers case studies, not iOS apps, so treat magnitude claims as directionally suggestive at best. Evidence quality across channels: TestFlight/waitlist = INFERENCE (Apple platform facts are FACT, but "it prevents cold starts" is a claim from a launch-tooling vendor, not a study); Product Hunt = ANECDOTE (repeated but self-selected); short-form video = ANECDOTE (a few named creators with specific numbers); build-in-public/niche communities = ANECDOTE; press outreach and launch offer codes = UNKNOWN (no concrete sourced example found in this pass).

### Cited Findings
- TestFlight supports up to 10,000 external testers per app, confirmed independently by both Apple's own App Analytics documentation (in the crash-rate improvement tip) and third-party guides. — FACT — [Apple Developer, App Analytics](https://developer.apple.com/app-store-connect/analytics/); consistent with [foresightmobile.com iOS distribution guide](https://foresightmobile.com/blog/ios-app-distribution-guide-2026)
- A pre-launch waitlist is argued (by a waitlist-tool vendor) to let a developer "recruit TestFlight testers, validate demand, and create launch-day momentum," giving "a launch-day email list and a referral engine" that avoids the typical indie cold-start problem — this is vendor argumentation, not independently measured, but the underlying TestFlight mechanics it relies on are factual. — INFERENCE/vendor claim — [getlaunchlist.com](https://getlaunchlist.com/waitlist-for-mobile-apps)
- Product Hunt: a solo Mac-utility developer's dev.to postmortem frames Product Hunt's audience as "tech-forward, early adopter, willing to try new tools" and reports launching three apps there with "consistent enough" results to describe a pattern (specific numbers not captured in this pass's snippet). Indie Hackers threads separately credit Product Hunt launches with reaching 100+ users "in a few hours" for at least one specific app (FocuSee, a screen-recording app that won 2nd Product of the Day). — ANECDOTE — [dev.to Product Hunt postmortem](https://dev.to/hiyoyok/product-hunt-launch-for-an-indie-mac-app-what-i-did-and-what-happened-5d6f); [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/indie-hackers-share-how-they-got-their-first-10-100-and-1-000-customers-620ce768ba)
- Reddit, in aggregate across multiple Indie Hackers respondents, is repeatedly named as the single most-credited channel for first 100+ customers, ahead of Twitter/X, LinkedIn, and Facebook groups; one respondent reports "14 registrations from a single subreddit post." — ANECDOTE (multiple independent but self-selected reports) — [Indie Hackers thread](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-where-did-you-find-your-first-1-10-and-100-customer-f146e8e3d3)
- Short-form video: a named YouTube case study ("The TikTok Organic Strategy That Led to 320K App Downloads," App Masters, 2023) and a separate creator (Adam Lyttle, "I grew my indie app to 30k+ downloads/month without ads") both report large organic download volumes attributed to TikTok/short-form content, without independent verification of the attribution methodology. — ANECDOTE — [App Masters video reference via search](https://www.youtube.com); [Adam Lyttle video reference via search](https://www.youtube.com)
- Launch-timing advice repeated across sources: concentrate announcements (Product Hunt + community posts + any press/newsletter coverage) into one short window rather than spreading them over weeks, because "launch momentum is about density, not duration." — INFERENCE (from a launch-strategy guide, not a controlled study) — [applaunchflow.com 2026 playbook](https://www.applaunchflow.com/blog/app-launch-strategy-2026)
- Self-promotion rules on niche communities, press outreach specifics, and launch offer codes: no concrete, sourced, named example with numbers was found in the searches run in this pass. — UNKNOWN

### Inferences
- Given that most "first 100 customers" success anecdotes come from SaaS/web products on Indie Hackers rather than App Store subscription apps, a solo iOS developer should discount the specific magnitudes (e.g., "100 in a few hours") and instead take the *channel selection* pattern (owned audience + Reddit + Product Hunt, concentrated in one launch window) as the more transferable lesson.
- The repeated, independently-sourced emphasis on "already having an audience/channel before launch" (Section 3) plus the TestFlight/waitlist mechanism (Section 4) together suggest the single highest-leverage AGENT-CAN-DO pre-launch action for a template/checklist is: stand up a TestFlight build and a waitlist signup page well before submission, not as an afterthought after App Store approval.

### Gaps
- No study quantifies relative channel effectiveness (e.g., conversion-to-paid-subscriber rate by acquisition channel) specifically for App Store subscription apps; all channel evidence here is about raw downloads/signups, not paying-subscriber outcomes.
- Press outreach and launch offer/promo codes as a channel: not substantiated with any concrete solo-developer example in this pass; would need a dedicated search (e.g., "indie iOS app press outreach TechCrunch pitch results") not completed given tool-budget limits.

## 5. Paywall and pricing choices with evidence

### Takeaway
The best-sourced, most decision-relevant paywall research (Adapty's 2026 "high-performing paywall" analysis, built on the same 16,000-app/$3B panel as Section 1) argues for six structural decisions ahead of any visual/copy tweaking: weekly-plan-plus-short-trial as the highest-LTV default for most categories (annual-first for Health & Fitness), trials that are selectively harmful in Productivity/Lifestyle/Graphics & Design, paywall placement after a value moment rather than immediately at open, deliberate (not default-low) pricing, hard-vs-soft paywall as a live A/B test rather than a one-time choice, and narrowly-targeted post-close discounts rather than broad discounting. Apple's own Guideline 3.1.2 sets a hard floor for "transparent, not dark-pattern" paywalls: continuing value is required, the minimum period is 7 days, and pricing/terms must be disclosed before the user is asked to subscribe — violations are a named, current rejection category.

### Cited Findings
- Weekly plans generated 43.3% of all app subscription revenue two years ago and 55.6%/56% in the 2025–2026 data — now the dominant plan type across most categories except Health & Fitness, where annual plans continue to grow share. — [Adapty high-performing-paywall analysis](https://adapty.io/blog/high-performing-paywall-2026/); consistent with [Adapty state-of-subscriptions report](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/)
- Best one-year-LTV configuration in the Adapty panel: weekly plan + 3-day free trial, at 1.5x the average LTV of all other configurations; worst: annual plan with no trial. Adding a trial to a weekly plan raises LTV from $7.40 to $54.50 (636% increase) in the report's headline example. — [Adapty high-performing-paywall analysis](https://adapty.io/blog/high-performing-paywall-2026/)
- Trials are *not* universally positive: in Productivity, direct buyers generate $56.95 one-year LTV vs. $49.13 for trial users; in Lifestyle, trial users are 21% less valuable than direct buyers; Graphics & Design shows the same pattern. Trials clearly help in Health & Fitness, Education, and Utilities, where users need time to experience value. — [Adapty high-performing-paywall analysis](https://adapty.io/blog/high-performing-paywall-2026/)
- Placement: onboarding paywalls with a trial convert at 1.35% on average — the highest of any placement/trial combination measured — and 90% of trial starts and 44.5% of all purchases happen on day 0, meaning a user who doesn't convert during onboarding rarely returns to convert later. — [Adapty high-performing-paywall analysis](https://adapty.io/blog/high-performing-paywall-2026/)
- A separate, independently-timed Adapty finding: paywalls placed after a measurable "value moment" (rather than immediately at onboarding) show trial-start rates 2.1x higher than immediate hard paywalls — this appears to sit in tension with the "onboarding paywall with trial converts best" finding above, and the note explicitly flags this as an area where the two Adapty analyses emphasize different cuts of the data (placement-within-onboarding vs. onboarding-vs.-later); a solo developer should A/B test rather than assume either finding transfers directly. — [Adapty via RocketShipHQ summary](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/adapty-subscription-app-benchmark-2025-summary/)
- Pricing: high-priced apps earn 3x the LTV of low-priced apps overall; in Health & Fitness specifically, expensive annual plans earn 4.5x more per user than cheap ones. Price increases rarely improve raw conversion (28.3% win rate on conversion in Adapty's own experiment data) but lift LTV in 45.5% of tested cases — i.e., higher prices bring in fewer but more valuable users, not necessarily more revenue-positive experiments overall. — [Adapty high-performing-paywall analysis](https://adapty.io/blog/high-performing-paywall-2026/)
- Hard vs. soft paywalls: hard paywalls produce 21% higher LTV; soft paywalls convert ~50% better (more top-of-funnel users, including some who'd never convert under a hard gate); the lowest-spending users pay about the same either way — Adapty explicitly frames "pick one forever" as the wrong approach and recommends testing both. — [Adapty high-performing-paywall analysis](https://adapty.io/blog/high-performing-paywall-2026/)
- Discounting: 9 in 10 subscriptions in the panel sell at full price; only 6.9% of subscriptions use a discount at all. The pattern found to work is a narrowly-targeted, time-limited (e.g., 24-hour) offer shown only to users who closed the onboarding paywall without converting, with an expected ~10–15% ARPU impact — broad discounting on the main paywall is explicitly called out as training all users to expect a lower price. — [Adapty high-performing-paywall analysis](https://adapty.io/blog/high-performing-paywall-2026/)
- Localization/price-by-region is the single highest-win-rate experiment category in Adapty's data (62.3% win rate on LTV, ahead of trial-structure tests at 59.6% and price-change tests at 45.5%), yet is described as "chronically underrated" and usually deprioritized behind visual/copy tests (which have the lowest win rate, 34.6%). European subscription prices rose ~18–40% year-over-year across the cited sources, making Europe now more expensive than North America on annual, monthly, and weekly plans in this panel. — [Adapty high-performing-paywall analysis](https://adapty.io/blog/high-performing-paywall-2026/)
- Web checkout vs. in-app: in-app paywalls convert 45% better than web paywalls (1.60% vs. 1.10%) and produce higher LTV ($40.10 vs. $35.80), even after accounting for Apple's commission — but web checkout is being tested by more apps in 2026 as an Android/web-to-app acquisition lever, not a superior primary iOS paywall. — [Adapty high-performing-paywall analysis](https://adapty.io/blog/high-performing-paywall-2026/)
- Apple's App Review Guideline 3.1.2(a): an auto-renewable subscription "must provide ongoing value to the customer, and the subscription period must last at least seven days," and apps "that attempt to trick users into purchasing a subscription under false pretenses or engage in bait-and-switch and scam practices" will be removed from the App Store (and the developer may be removed from the Developer Program). — FACT (primary source) — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Guideline 3.1.2(c): "Before asking a customer to subscribe, you should clearly describe what the user will get for the price," specifically calling out clear communication of quantity/access/duration and referencing Schedule 2 of the Apple Developer Program License Agreement for required disclosures — this is the textual basis for "toggle paywall" and other pre-checked/obscured-price rejections reported by ASO/paywall vendors in 2025–2026. — FACT (primary source) — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Apple's own win-back offers feature (native, in App Store Connect) is explicitly positioned by Apple as a tool "to re-acquire churned subscribers of an auto-renewable subscription," and Apple separately documents a newer Retention Messaging capability (previewed at WWDC 2026) to reach subscribers with in-the-moment messaging before they cancel. — FACT (primary source) — [Apple Developer, win-back offers](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appstoreconnectapi); WWDC26 session referenced via [Apple Developer YouTube listing, found via search](https://developer.apple.com)

### Inferences
- The apparent tension between "onboarding-paywall-with-trial converts best" and "paywall-after-a-value-moment gets 2.1x more trial starts" most plausibly resolves as: *placing the paywall inside/at-the-end-of onboarding* (rather than immediately on cold open, before any value has been shown) is what both findings actually support — "onboarding paywall" in the first study likely already means "after a short value-demonstrating onboarding flow," not "before any screen at all." A solo developer should read both as arguing against a bare, pre-onboarding hard paywall, and should A/B test placement rather than assume either citation's exact number transfers.
- Given Guideline 3.1.2(c)'s explicit disclosure requirement and the 2026 crackdown on "toggle paywalls" reported by multiple ASO vendors (Volpis, Adapty, RevenueFlo — all secondary/vendor sources, not independently verified in this pass beyond the Google-snippet level), a transparent paywall (clear price, clear trial length, clear renewal terms, no pre-selected upsell toggle that obscures the real charge) is now both the ethically preferable and the App-Review-safer choice; "dark pattern" paywall tricks are a live rejection risk in 2026, not a settled/tolerated gray area.

### Gaps
- The exact 2025 "toggle paywall" Guideline 3.1.2 rejection wave (referenced by Adapty and others in Google-search snippets) could not be verified against Apple's own guideline text beyond the general disclosure language quoted above — no primary Apple statement naming "toggle paywalls" specifically was found in this pass; treat the "crackdown" framing as vendor-reported, not Apple-confirmed.
- No study in this pass measured monthly-first vs. annual-first *as a launch default* (as distinct from trial length or hard/soft paywall) with a clean A/B; the closest evidence is the plan-revenue-share shift toward weekly (Section 5 above) and the renewal-rate-by-duration table (Section 1), which are suggestive but not a controlled comparison.

## 6. ASO in 2026

### Takeaway
Apple's own product-page mechanics are unchanged in their basic limits (30-character title, 30-character subtitle, 100-character comma-separated keyword field) but the discoverability surface has expanded materially: Custom Product Pages (up to 70 additional pages) can now carry their *own* assigned keywords to make that specific page separately discoverable in search — a genuine 2025-era capability, not just a conversion-optimization tool — and cross-locale keyword indexing (e.g., a Spanish-Mexico localization's keywords being indexed for the US storefront) is a well-documented practitioner technique for roughly doubling effective keyword coverage on a single storefront, though it is not something Apple itself documents in those terms.

### Cited Findings
- App name: up to 30 characters. Subtitle: up to 30 characters, appears below the app name throughout the store, and can be changed with every version submission. Keywords: 100 characters total, comma-separated, no spaces needed between the comma and next term unless within a multi-word phrase; avoid plurals of singular terms already included, category names, the word "app," duplicate words, and non-brand special characters, none of which "carry extra weight." Promotional text (170 characters) does *not* affect search ranking and should not be used to stuff keywords. — FACT (primary source) — [Apple Developer, Creating Your Product Page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)
- Screenshots: up to 10 per product page; the first one to three (depending on orientation) appear directly in search results when no app preview is present, so they carry outsized weight. App previews (video): up to 3 per page, up to 30 seconds each, localizable, autoplay muted. — FACT (primary source) — [Apple Developer, Creating Your Product Page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)
- Ratings prompts: the native SKStoreReviewController API allows a developer to prompt for a rating up to three times within any 365-day period; users can write/submit a full review without leaving the app. — FACT (primary source) — [Apple Developer, Creating Your Product Page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)
- Categories: one primary and one secondary category per app; the primary category drives both search-filter matching and which App Store tab the app appears under, and choosing an irrelevant category is an explicit rejection risk. — FACT (primary source) — [Apple Developer, Creating Your Product Page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)
- Localization: description, keywords, previews, and screenshots should all be localized per target market; app name and keyword emphasis can also be tailored per locale. — FACT (primary source) — [Apple Developer, Creating Your Product Page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)
- Product Page Optimization (PPO): built into App Store Connect; lets a developer show alternate icon/screenshot/preview treatments to a random slice of eligible visitors and read results in App Analytics before rolling the winner out to everyone. — FACT (primary source) — [Apple Developer, Creating Your Product Page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)
- Custom Product Pages: up to 70 additional product-page versions per app (iPhone/iPad), each independently localizable, each able to carry its own screenshots/previews/promotional text/deep link, and — critically — each able to be assigned its own keywords so that page (not just the default page) appears in relevant search results, provided each keyword combination is unique to a single page. Apple reports developers see an average 2.5-percentage-point conversion lift referring traffic to a Custom Product Page vs. the 1.6% average default-page conversion rate — a 156% relative increase; named case studies include CBS Sports (20% conversion) and State of Survival (33% conversion, 14% lower cost-per-install) when used with Apple Ads. — FACT (primary source) — [Apple Developer, Custom Product Pages](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/custom-product-pages/)
- In-App Events: up to 15 approved events at a time in App Store Connect, up to 10 published simultaneously on the store; events can run up to 31 days and be promoted up to 14 days ahead of their start; event name ≤30 characters, short description ≤50 characters, long description ≤120 characters; events surface in the app's product page, in search results (as an event card alongside the app, or directly searchable by event name), and in editorial/personalized placements. — FACT (primary source) — [Apple Developer, In-App Events](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/in-app-events/)
- Featuring Nominations: submitted via App Store Connect; Apple asks for a minimum of two weeks' notice, and recommends submitting up to three months ahead for wider consideration; there is explicitly "no checklist of requirements," but named evaluation factors include user experience, UI design, innovation, uniqueness, accessibility, localization quality, and the product page itself (screenshots, previews, descriptions, ratings/reviews). — FACT (primary source) — [Apple Developer, Getting Featured](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/getting-featured/)
- App Store Connect Analytics 2025–2026 overhaul (Section 8 detail applies here too): peer group benchmarks now include download-to-paid conversion and proceeds-per-download, both directly relevant to judging whether an ASO/paywall change is working relative to similar apps. — FACT (primary source) — [Apple Developer, App Analytics](https://developer.apple.com/app-store-connect/analytics/)
- Cross-storefront keyword indexing: multiple independent ASO-practitioner sources (MobileAction, AppTweak, SplitMetrics, and corroborating Reddit r/AppStoreOptimization threads, none of which is Apple itself) consistently report that Apple indexes keywords from more than one locale for a single storefront — e.g., the US App Store search indexes both the primary English (U.S.) localization's keywords *and* the Spanish (Mexico) localization's keywords — letting a developer who fills in both localizations effectively field two 100-character keyword fields against the same US audience. — practitioner consensus, not Apple-documented — [MobileAction](https://www.mobileaction.co/aso); [SplitMetrics](https://splitmetrics.com); cross-checked against multiple independent secondary sources in the same search pass

### Inferences
- Because Custom Product Pages can now carry independently-targeted keywords (not just independently-targeted creative), a solo developer's ASO plan should treat CPPs as a second discoverability channel, not merely a conversion-rate-optimization or ad-landing-page tool — e.g., a translation/study app could run one default page targeting its core term and a second CPP targeting a specific adjacent search term, subject to Apple's requirement that each keyword combination map to a single unique page.
- Given that the first 1–3 screenshots substitute for a video preview directly in search results, and that PPO plus Custom Product Pages both report through the same Acquisition-tab conversion-rate metric, a solo developer with limited design time should prioritize testing the first screenshot over testing keyword-field wording, since the screenshot affects both search-result click-through and on-page conversion simultaneously.

### Gaps
- No primary Apple document was found that explicitly names or confirms the cross-locale/cross-storefront keyword indexing behavior described above; it remains third-party practitioner knowledge (widely repeated, cross-corroborated by several unaffiliated ASO tool vendors, but not Apple-confirmed) — flag this explicitly wherever it's cited downstream.
- The precise 2025 change date/version for Custom Product Pages gaining keyword-assignment capability (as opposed to only creative-swap capability) was not pinned to a specific dated Apple release note in this pass; the current Apple page describes it as a present-tense capability without a changelog date.

## 7. Apple Ads (formerly Search Ads): Basic vs Advanced, minimums, typical CPT/CPI, whether buying first customers makes sense

### Takeaway
Apple Ads (rebranded from "Apple Search Ads") splits into a self-serve, automation-heavy Basic tier (built-in $100 trial credit, pay-only-for-installs at a developer-set max CPI, pause any time) and a manual/expert Advanced tier (cost-per-tap bidding, multiple placements — Today tab, Search tab, Search Results, Product Pages — and full API access). Typical costs for a small, non-competitive niche in 2026 are commonly reported in the roughly $0.50–$2.25 cost-per-tap / cost-per-install range depending heavily on country and category (with the US among the most expensive markets and several developing markets over 10x cheaper), and Apple's own claimed >60% search-ad conversion rate plus "95% of downloads occur within a minute of an ad tap" data suggest paid Apple Ads traffic converts unusually fast and at high intent — making a small, tightly-targeted Apple Ads Basic campaign a plausible, low-commitment way to buy a first cohort of real users, though no source found in this pass specifically confirms it reliably buys a first *paying subscriber* rather than just an install.

### Cited Findings
- Apple Ads Basic: self-serve, automated bidding to a developer-chosen max cost-per-install and monthly budget; can be started or stopped any time with no long-term commitment; new advertisers get a $100 USD credit to trial it. — FACT (primary source) — [Apple Ads Basic](https://ads.apple.com/en/app-store/basic)
- Apple Ads Advanced: manual or "Maximize Conversions" (AI-assisted) bidding on cost-per-tap; ad placements across the Today tab, Search tab, Search Results, and Product Pages; supports Custom Product Pages as ad landing destinations and deep links; same $100 trial credit is offered. — FACT (primary source) — [Apple Ads, App Store overview](https://ads.apple.com/app-store)
- Apple's own headline stats: over 850 million people visit the App Store weekly; 70% of App Store visitors use Search to discover apps; almost 65% of downloads happen directly after a search; ads at the top of Search Results convert at an average >60%; 95% of downloads after an ad tap occur within a minute of the tap. — FACT (primary source, Apple's own marketing claim — note these are Apple's self-reported aggregate figures, not independently audited) — [Apple Ads, App Store overview](https://ads.apple.com/app-store)
- Cost-per-tap by country (Adapty's 2026 Apple Ads benchmark report, subscription-app-specific): US $1.576, UK $1.308, China $1.254, Australia $1.170, Israel $1.005, Canada $0.957, Switzerland $0.945, Germany $0.858, Norway $0.755, France $0.747 — roughly a 2x spread among these ten most expensive markets alone, with the report's headline claiming a 14x spread between the most and least expensive markets overall (e.g., a US tap costs roughly 14x an Algerian tap in the cited framing). — [Adapty Apple Ads benchmarks](https://adapty.io/apple-ads-for-subscription-apps/) (exact sample size/app count for this specific report was displayed as an animated counter that did not render in static text extraction — treat the underlying "N apps / N countries / N niches" claim as present but unconfirmed in magnitude)
- Median cost-per-install across 90 markets in the same Adapty report: $0.51; ad-spend-weighted blended average: $1.34 (i.e., higher-spend campaigns cluster in more expensive markets, pulling the spend-weighted mean well above the simple median). — [Adapty Apple Ads benchmarks](https://adapty.io/apple-ads-for-subscription-apps/)
- Apple Ads converts to a *paying* subscriber roughly 2.1–2.5x better than other paid channels in the same panel (install→trial 3.66% vs. 1.45% for other paid; install→paid 1.92% vs. 0.91%), and the install-to-paid advantage is much larger in some markets (e.g., +394% in Indonesia, +383% in South Africa) than others (e.g., a smaller lift in mature Western markets). — [Adapty Apple Ads benchmarks](https://adapty.io/apple-ads-for-subscription-apps/)
- Top-converting niches by conversion rate in the same report: PDF Reader (77.9% CR, $4.31 CPA), Remote Control (77.6% CR, $0.56 CPA), Screen Recorder (76.3% CR, $0.34 CPA); median CR across 59 sub-niches measured was 61.25%. — [Adapty Apple Ads benchmarks](https://adapty.io/apple-ads-for-subscription-apps/)
- Independent cross-checks from other secondary sources found via search (not independently fetched/verified in full in this pass) put CPT ranges roughly consistent with the above: SplitMetrics reported a top-15-category average CPT of $2.25 in 2025; AppTweak reported median CPT by country (US $1.91, UK $1.35, Canada $1.17, Australia $1.15) for search-results campaigns; a separate practitioner estimate suggested a $2.50–$3.00 average cost-per-install with a ~66% conversion rate. These broadly corroborate Adapty's figures within the same order of magnitude, though exact numbers differ by source/dataset/date — treat the true figure as "roughly $1–2.50 CPT / $0.50–3 CPI depending on market and category," not a single number. — secondary sources found via search snippet, not independently verified — [SplitMetrics](https://splitmetrics.com), [AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com), figures as surfaced in a Google search results page

### Inferences
- Given Apple Ads' reported ~2x-better install-to-paid conversion vs. other paid channels (Adapty), a $100 free-credit Apple Ads Basic campaign, tightly scoped to a low-competition niche keyword where the developer's own app is a strong match, is a low-risk, bounded-cost way for a solo developer to generate the first handful of real, intent-driven installs — but because the free credit is small relative to even a single-digit-dollar CPT/CPI in competitive niches, it should be framed as "test the ad channel and paywall together, cheaply" rather than "acquire meaningful subscriber volume."
- Because 44–90% of a new app's trial-starts and purchases already happen on day 0 (Sections 2 and 5), and Apple Ads' own funnel shows extremely fast time-to-download after a tap (95% within a minute), an Apple Ads-driven first cohort will produce an unusually fast, concentrated read on paywall/onboarding performance — useful for quick iteration, but a small enough sample that a solo developer should not overfit paywall decisions to a handful of ad-driven early users.

### Gaps
- No official Apple-stated minimum daily/monthly budget figure for either Basic or Advanced was found on the pages reached in this pass (Apple's marketing pages emphasize flexibility — "set your own budget," "start or stop any time" — rather than publishing a hard minimum); a definitive minimum-spend figure would need to be checked directly inside App Store Connect / Apple Ads account setup, which requires an active developer account (HUMAN-ONLY / AGENT-CAN-OBSERVE at best, not fetchable from public marketing pages).
- No source found in this pass directly measures "does buying an Apple Ads first customer produce a subscriber who is retained/renews at a normal rate," as opposed to just converting to a first purchase — the Adapty install-to-paid data addresses first conversion, not first-renewal quality, for ad-acquired users specifically.

## 8. Measurement

### Takeaway
Apple significantly overhauled App Store Connect Analytics for 2025–2026, adding 100+ monetization/subscription metrics, cohort analysis (e.g., time-to-first-purchase by acquisition source or region), and privacy-preserving peer-group benchmarks for download-to-paid conversion and proceeds-per-download — this is now a genuinely useful, free, first-party measurement layer that didn't exist in this form before. For observing a literal "first renewal," the concrete, implementable signal is the App Store Server Notifications V2 `DID_RENEW` notification type (or the equivalent RevenueCat `RENEWAL` webhook/event if using RevenueCat as a metering layer) — not a dashboard metric a developer has to interpret, but a discrete server-to-server event a developer can log the first time it fires per subscriber. App Tracking Transparency (ATT) is only required when an app collects data and shares it with *other companies* to track a person/device across apps and websites — a solo developer using only first-party analytics (App Store Connect Analytics, or a subscription tool like RevenueCat that isn't sharing data cross-app for advertising) does not need to show the ATT prompt for that purpose alone; ATT/IDFA and SKAdNetwork/AdAttributionKit only become relevant once the developer is running attributed paid user-acquisition (e.g., Apple Ads with cross-app measurement, or third-party ad networks).

### Cited Findings
- App Store Connect Analytics 2025–2026 overhaul, confirmed on Apple's own current page: "More than 100 new metrics" for monetization/subscription performance; "New cohort capabilities" to analyze user behavior by shared attributes (download date, download source, offer start date, etc.) over time; "New peer group benchmarks" for download-to-paid conversion and proceeds-per-download; "Two new subscription reports" exportable via the Analytics Reports API; and support for up to seven simultaneous filters on any metric. — FACT (primary source) — [Apple Developer, App Analytics](https://developer.apple.com/app-store-connect/analytics/)
- Peer group benchmarks are constructed using App Store category, business model (an app must generate ≥50% of revenue from auto-renewable subscriptions to be grouped in the "subscription" business-model peer group), and download-volume tier (low/medium/high), with differential privacy applied and a minimum-apps-per-group threshold enforced "to ensure individual app performance remains protected"; Apple's own apps may be included in a peer group's benchmark pool. — FACT (primary source) — [Apple Developer, App Analytics](https://developer.apple.com/app-store-connect/analytics/)
- Conversion rate as defined by Apple: total downloads (first-time + redownloads) divided by unique product-page impressions in a given day — a directly actionable, first-party proxy for "how many people who saw my page installed," usable to judge ASO changes without any third-party SDK. — FACT (primary source) — [Apple Developer, App Analytics](https://developer.apple.com/app-store-connect/analytics/)
- Retention rate as defined by Apple: devices that opened the app in the days following download, divided by devices that downloaded it on that day and had at least one session in the last 30 days — Apple explicitly frames a low retention rate as a prompt to revisit onboarding. — FACT (primary source) — [Apple Developer, App Analytics](https://developer.apple.com/app-store-connect/analytics/)
- App Store Server Notifications and RevenueCat's equivalent: RevenueCat's RENEWAL event (the vendor-neutral equivalent to observing a "first renewal," corresponding to Apple's underlying `DID_RENEW`/renewal notification types) fires when "an existing subscription was renewed, or a lapsed user resubscribed," generated once the renewal payment is confirmed. This is the concrete signal a solo developer should log and alert on for their very first renewal event. — FACT (primary source) — [RevenueCat, event types and fields](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/integrations/webhooks/event-types-and-fields)
- App Tracking Transparency scope, per Apple's own framework documentation: "Your app needs to use the App Tracking Transparency framework if it collects data about people and shares it with other companies to track them across apps and websites" — i.e., ATT governs cross-company tracking use of the IDFA/device data, not first-party in-app analytics or first-party subscription-state tracking by itself. — FACT (primary source) — [Apple Developer, App Tracking Transparency](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/apptrackingtransparency)
- ATT opt-in rates remain low in aggregate: Business of Apps reports (headline stats only; full report is paywalled) that games have the highest ATT opt-in rate among app categories at 39%, hyper-casual games lead within gaming, the UAE has the highest opt-in rate by country at close to 50%, and fewer than 20% of apps overall track users via data-sharing (18% specifically request background location access) — implying that any ad-attribution strategy relying on IDFA-based tracking will only ever see a minority of the true user base directly, reinforcing SKAdNetwork/AdAttributionKit's role as the majority-case measurement path for paid UA. — FACT headline stats (report itself paywalled; percentages not independently re-derived) — [Business of Apps, ATT opt-in rates](https://www.businessofapps.com/data/att-opt-in-rates/)
- Apple's own guidance ties measurement directly to action: below-peer conversion rate → run Product Page Optimization or try Custom Product Pages or improve localization; below-average D35 download-to-paid rate → consider offering a free trial/item during onboarding; below-peer proceeds-per-paying-user → reassess Apple In-App Purchase pricing, including per-territory pricing. — FACT (primary source) — [Apple Developer, App Analytics](https://developer.apple.com/app-store-connect/analytics/)

### Inferences
- Because App Store Connect Analytics' peer-group benchmarks are now free, first-party, privacy-preserving, and specifically include a subscription-business-model peer group, a solo developer building a subscription app in 2026 has materially less reason than in prior years to install a third-party analytics SDK purely to get "how do I compare to similar apps" — that comparison is now built into Apple's own free tooling, which also has the side benefit of not needing ATT consent at all (Apple's own first-party analytics are not "sharing data with other companies").
- The combination of (a) ATT only being required for cross-company tracking and (b) RevenueCat's RENEWAL/App Store Server Notifications being a first-party, developer-owned webhook means a solo developer can build a complete "did my first subscriber renew" measurement pipeline (App Store Connect Analytics for acquisition/conversion + RevenueCat or raw App Store Server Notifications for the renewal event) without ever triggering the ATT prompt, as long as they are not also running IDFA-based ad attribution — a meaningful simplification for a pre-monetization-scale app that isn't yet running paid UA.

### Gaps
- No source in this pass gave the exact minimum peer-group size threshold Apple enforces before showing a benchmark (described only as "enough apps within that peer group to ensure individual app performance remains protected") — meaning a genuinely new, low-download-volume solo app may not see a peer benchmark populate at all in its early weeks; this operational detail (does a brand-new app actually see benchmark data before some downloads/time threshold) was not confirmed.
- SKAdNetwork/AdAttributionKit specifics (postback timing windows, conversion-value schema limits as of iOS 18/19) were not researched in this pass since the assignment scoped them to "only if running ads," and this note's Section 7 already establishes that a solo developer's first Apple Ads test would primarily rely on Apple's own first-party Apple Ads reporting (which does not require SKAdNetwork the way third-party ad networks do) rather than needing a deep SKAdNetwork implementation on day one.
