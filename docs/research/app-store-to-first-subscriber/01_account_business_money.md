# App Store: Account, Business, Tax, Banking, Commission, Payout Setup for a Solo Developer's First Subscription

Current as of 2026-09-27. Assumes a US-based solo/individual developer unless noted. Every claim is tagged FACT (with URL and, where the page states one, its "reviewed/updated" date), INFERENCE, or UNKNOWN/GAP. Every actionable step is tagged with an owner (HUMAN-ONLY / AGENT-CAN-DO / AGENT-CAN-OBSERVE), a lead time, and a failure mode where known. **Not legal or tax advice** — flagged explicitly wherever a CPA/attorney is warranted.

## 1. Paid Applications Agreement (Schedule 2) vs Program License Agreement

### Takeaway
Joining the Apple Developer Program only signs the free-distribution **Program License Agreement (PLA)**. Selling anything — a paid app or any In-App Purchase, including a subscription — requires the **Paid Applications Agreement (Schedule 2)**, which **only the Account Holder role can sign**, and which has its own multi-stage status machine. Until that agreement reads "Active," you cannot even *create* a new IAP/subscription product in App Store Connect, let alone sell it — this is the single most blocking gate in the entire sequence.

### Cited Findings
- FACT — "By joining the Apple Developer Program, you can distribute free apps on the App Store under the Apple Developer Program License Agreement. To sell your apps on the App Store or offer In-App Purchases, the Account Holder must sign the Paid Apps Agreement. **Required role: Account Holder.**" — [Sign and update agreements](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-agreements/sign-and-update-agreements) (Apple Developer, App Store Connect Help)
- FACT — "You won't be able to create a new app or In-App Purchase until you've agreed to the most recent version of the Paid Apps Agreement." — same source. This directly answers "what's blocked": not just sale, but *creation* of the IAP/subscription product record itself.
- FACT — Full agreement status list and definitions — [View agreements status](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-agreements/view-agreements-status):
  | Status | Meaning |
  |---|---|
  | New | Not yet signed |
  | Pending User Info | Signed, but required info (tax/banking) not yet complete |
  | Processing | Info complete, Apple reviewing |
  | Verifying | Additional legal-entity info under review |
  | Active | In effect |
  | Active (Pending User) | In effect, but some info still outstanding |
  | Active (New Agreement Available) | In effect; a newer version is available to sign |
  | Pending (New Legal Entity) | Legal entity changed; must sign a new contract |
  | Pending (Update Legal Entity) | Legal entity changed; new banking/tax forms required |
  | Pending (New Agreement Available) | Newer version of a pending agreement is available |
  | Expired | Agreement has expired |
  | Disabled | **App is removed from the App Store** until required info is provided and accepted |
- FACT — Viewing/managing agreement status requires role Account Holder, Admin, or Finance, but *signing* requires Account Holder specifically (per Sign and update agreements page above) — [View agreements status](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-agreements/view-agreements-status).
- FACT (renewal case) — "If you renew your membership after it expires, you'll need to agree to the Paid Apps Agreement again. This agreement must be active in order for you to submit or update paid apps and In-App Purchases." — [Sign and update agreements](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-agreements/sign-and-update-agreements)
- INFERENCE (secondary, ptkd.com blog, unverified against a primary Apple SLA) — Once tax forms and banking info are submitted after signing, the agreement "activates on its own, typically within about a day" — [PTKD Journal](https://ptkd.com/journal/app-store-connect-pending-agreement-tax-forms). Treat as anecdotal, not an Apple-committed SLA.
- FACT — Free apps with no In-App Purchases are unaffected by a non-Active Paid Apps Agreement — this is why the gap is often discovered only when a developer first tries to monetize (derived from the "New app or In-App Purchase" blocking language above; free-app creation is governed by the PLA only).

### Inferences
- INFERENCE — Because App Store Connect will not let you *create* the IAP/subscription product record without an Active (or at least a signed/processing, non-New) Paid Apps Agreement, StoreKit sandbox testing is also blocked in practice during "New"/"Pending User Info" — there is nothing to test yet. I could not find an Apple page stating this explicitly for the "Processing" state specifically (i.e., whether a product created while Processing can be sandbox-tested before the agreement flips to Active). Treat the exact boundary between "Processing" and full sandbox availability as unconfirmed.
- INFERENCE — Practically, a solo developer should sign Schedule 2 and submit tax/banking information in the very first session of setting up the developer account, well before writing StoreKit code, because the product-creation block (not just the sale block) is what silently derails a subscription feature built too early.

### Gaps
- GAP — No primary Apple source found with an explicit committed turnaround time (in days) from "Processing" to "Active" for Schedule 2; the "~1 day" figure is a single secondary/anecdotal source.
- GAP — No explicit Apple documentation confirming/denying that StoreKit **sandbox** purchases specifically (as opposed to submitting to App Review) are blocked while the agreement is Pending/Processing. Recommend the report flag this as unverified and advise developers to treat "no Active agreement" as "assume sandbox IAP is unavailable" until proven otherwise.

**Owner / lead time / failure mode:** Signing Schedule 2 — **HUMAN-ONLY** (binding legal agreement; must be the Account Holder's own authenticated session). Lead time: minutes to sign, then Processing until tax+banking complete (see §2) — realistically **1–3+ days** if tax/banking are submitted same-day, longer if verification stalls. Failure mode if skipped: cannot create the subscription product at all; a StoreKit integration built against a product ID that doesn't exist yet will simply fail to load products from the App Store, which is often misdiagnosed as a code bug rather than an agreements/business gate.

## 2. Tax and banking (W-9/W-8BEN, bank account, verification, incomplete-info consequences)

### Takeaway
US individuals fill a **W-9** (SSN/ITIN, or EIN if a business entity); non-US individuals/entities fill a **W-8BEN/W-8BEN-E/W-8ECI** determined via a guided questionnaire. Missing or incomplete tax info, or missing banking info, delays or withholds payment — Apple states this per-country (e.g., South Korea) but the general principle (no valid tax form + no valid bank info = no payment) holds across the platform. Once submitted, tax info **cannot be edited directly in App Store Connect** — corrections require contacting Apple.

### Cited Findings
- FACT — "If you're based in the United States, you're prompted to complete a W-9 form. If you're based outside the United States, the W-8BEN, W-8BEN-E, or W-8ECI may be required... you'll be prompted to answer a series of questions to direct you to the most appropriate tax form." — [Provide tax information](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-tax-information/provide-tax-information/) (App Store Connect Help)
- FACT — TIN for an individual = SSN (which doubles as ITIN); TIN for a business entity = EIN — same source.
- FACT — Treaty benefits require a tax residency certificate; without one, sales may be "subject to the applicable withholding tax at the standard rate" (Apple's own example is Brazil) — same source.
- FACT — "Once you submit this information, you won't be able to make any changes in App Store Connect. For any corrections or additional tax forms, [contact us]." — same source.
- FACT — Country-specific extra registration requirements exist and gate payment/invoicing: Australia (GST registration mandatory to sell apps), Canada (GST/HST, QST), Ireland (VAT ID), Mexico (VAT registration/withholding), Singapore (GST number or non-registered status), South Korea (Business Registration Number/NTS), Taiwan (TIN), Thailand (VAT number/non-registered status) — same source.
- FACT (South Korea specific, illustrating the general "incomplete tax info" consequence) — "Failure to provide an up to date tax information may result in a delay of payment on your earnings and not receiving e-Tax invoices." — [Manage invoices and other tax documents](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-tax-information/manage-invoices-and-other-tax-documents/)
- FACT — Requirements to receive any payment at all: an **active Paid Applications Agreement**, submitted **tax information**, and **banking information** — synthesized directly from the same Apple help section structure (tax info page + agreements page); all three are independently gating.
- INFERENCE (secondary, from search-engine synthesis of Apple's own tax-info page, not independently re-verified this session by direct fetch of a "default US withholding rate" statement) — Because the App Store agreement is a "sales/commission" arrangement rather than a "royalty" arrangement, US-source sales generally are **not** subject to US withholding tax for the developer regardless of residency — this is a claim worth flagging for professional confirmation, as I could not independently verify an explicit percentage or clause on an Apple-owned page in this session.

### Inferences
- INFERENCE — The three gates (agreement Active, tax form accepted, banking accepted) are independent AND-conditions: satisfying two of three still yields zero payout. A developer who signs Schedule 2 and adds a bank account but skips the tax interview will still see no proceeds, with the failure surfacing only as a silently-stuck "Processing" agreement status rather than a clear "payment blocked" message.
- INFERENCE — Because tax info is locked after submission, a solo developer who later forms an LLC and wants to switch from W-9 (SSN) to W-9 (EIN) cannot self-serve the correction; it requires an Apple support ticket and likely triggers a new Pending Apps Agreement cycle (see §3, "Updating your account information").

### Gaps
- GAP — No specific numeric lead time found for **bank account verification** itself (as distinct from the tax-form step or the overall agreement activation). Apple's pages describe *what* is required, not a committed number of business days for bank verification specifically.
- GAP — No confirmed default withholding *percentage* found on an Apple-owned page for a non-US developer with no treaty certificate on file, selling into the US storefront specifically (only the general "standard rate" language, illustrated for Brazil, was found).

**Owner / lead time / failure mode:** Entering SSN/EIN/bank account/routing numbers into App Store Connect is squarely **HUMAN-ONLY** (financial credential/PII entry is a prohibited agent action regardless of context). An agent can only **observe** the resulting agreement/tax/banking status in App Store Connect and tell the human what's missing. Lead time: the human-facing form itself is minutes; downstream Apple review/activation is the 1–3+ day variable from §1. Failure mode: incomplete tax OR banking info silently holds all proceeds even after the app is live and selling — money accrues in Apple's ledger but is never deposited until all three gates clear.

## 3. Apple Developer Program: fee, individual vs organization, D-U-N-S, identity verification, minors, 2FA, seller name, conversion, transfer, lapse

### Takeaway
The Program costs **$99/year regardless of individual or organization** enrollment type (Enterprise is $299/year, a different program). Individuals need only a legally-named Apple Account with 2FA and no D-U-N-S; organizations need a D-U-N-S Number (free, but up to **7 business days** total lead time), a public organization-domain website and email, and an Account Holder with **legal binding authority**. The seller name shown publicly is your **personal legal name** for individuals (no DBA/alias allowed) or your **legal entity's registered name** for organizations. Converting individual→organization is a support-mediated, unpredictable-duration process; transferring an *app* between separate accounts has a strict, documented eligibility checklist. A lapsed membership pulls apps from sale but does **not** interrupt subscriptions already held by existing subscribers.

### Cited Findings
- FACT — Apple Developer Program: **$99 USD/year** (local-currency equivalent); Apple Developer Enterprise Program: **$299 USD/year**; same price for individual and organization membership — [Become a member](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/)
- FACT — Fee waivers available for nonprofit organizations, accredited educational institutions, or government entities that meet certain requirements, requested during enrollment — same source.
- FACT — Individual enrollment requirements: Apple Account with 2FA on; legal age of majority in your region; **legal name only** (no alias/nickname/company name) in first/last name fields — "Using an alias, nickname, or company name... will cause a delay in the approval of your enrollment"; confirmed items include legal name (displayed as **seller name** on the App Store), email, phone, and a street address (**no P.O. boxes**) — [Become a member](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/)
- FACT — Organization enrollment requirements: Apple Account with 2FA (recommend org email); **legal binding authority** — the Account Holder "must be the organization's owner/founder, executive team member, senior project lead, or an employee with legal authority granted... by a senior employee"; legal entity name/status (**DBAs, fictitious business names, trade names, branches not accepted**) shown as seller name; **D-U-N-S Number** (except government entities); work email tied to the org's domain; a **publicly available and functional website** whose domain is associated with the organization (social-media-only pages or registrar placeholder pages rejected) — same source.
- FACT — D-U-N-S Number: free from Dun & Bradstreet; not needed for individuals/sole proprietors; required for companies and educational institutions; optional for government orgs; not accepted for DBAs/fictitious businesses/trade names/branches. Lead time: **up to 5 business days** for D&B to issue the number, plus **up to 2 business days** for Apple to receive the updated D&B data — **up to 7 business days total** before you can even enroll as an org. Expediting does not shorten this. If your business shows as a sole proprietorship (not a "legal entity" like a corporation, LLC, or limited partnership) in D&B's database, you must enroll as an individual instead. — [D-U-N-S Number](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/membership/D-U-N-S/)
- FACT — Individual/sole-proprietor seller-name rule: "If you are a sole proprietor/single-person business, you must join as an individual and your legal name will appear as the seller"; "You cannot use a DBA (Doing Business As), business name, or any made-up name instead of your legal name." Only enrolling as an organization (with a genuine legal entity) allows a business name to display as seller — [Become a member](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/) + [D-U-N-S Number](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/membership/D-U-N-S/)
- FACT — Individual→organization conversion: "If you'd like to update your membership from an individual to an organization, please submit a request. You'll need to be the founder/cofounder of the organization and provide details such as your organization's D-U-N-S Number. You also may be asked to submit business documents to verify your organization's identity." — [Updating your account information](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/membership/updating-your-account-information/)
- FACT — Account Holder role transfer: an organization's Account Holder can transfer the role to another employee with legal binding authority; "Account Holder transfers for individual members are granted on a limited basis and require assistance from Apple" — same source.
- FACT — Changing an organization's registered name updates the vendor name across all apps, **resets identifierForVendor (IDFV)** for existing users on their next update, can cause mobile-measurement partners to double-count installs, and "cannot be undone" — same source. This is a concrete silent-break risk if a solo dev incorporates later and renames the seller entity.
- INFERENCE/anecdotal (Apple Developer Forums, multiple threads, not an official SLA) — Real-world individual→organization conversion requests have taken "nearly one month" or longer, and at least one thread reports Apple **denying** the migration request outright and rejecting the app as a result — [Forums thread example](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/) (aggregated from Google-indexed forum titles: "Individual to Organization migration denied, app rejected", "Still Waiting for Apple Developer Account Conversion"). Treat lead time here as **unpredictable and potentially weeks**, not a guaranteed short process.
- FACT — App **transfer** (moving an app to a different, separate developer account) eligibility criteria — [App transfer criteria](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/transfer-an-app/app-transfer-criteria/):
  - Both transferor's and recipient's accounts must not be in a pending/changing state, and both must have accepted the latest paid and free agreements (and the EU Alternative Terms Addendum, if the transferor accepted it).
  - App must have at least one version released to the App Store; cannot be in Processing for Distribution, Waiting for Review, In Review, Accepted, Pending Developer Release, or Pending Apple Release; cannot be available for pre-order.
  - In-App Purchase products must be Approved, Ready to Submit, Developer Removed from Sale, or Rejected.
  - IAP product IDs on the app can't collide with product IDs already in the recipient's account.
  - Mac apps that share an Application Group Container Directory via sandbox can't transfer; Apple Arcade apps can't transfer.
  - Recipient must accept the transfer within **60 days** of initiation.
- FACT — Lapsed/expired standard Apple Developer Program membership: app is pulled from sale/download; new submissions and updates blocked; Certificates/Identifiers/Profiles access blocked; **already-installed apps keep working for existing users**; **users with existing active in-app subscriptions retain access** — the lapse does not cut off current subscribers. Upon renewal: free apps become available again within 24 hours; paid apps require re-accepting the Paid Apps Agreement; no resubmission needed. Enterprise Program lapse has a **90-day grace period** for already-downloaded software. Device-list auto-removal occurs 180 days after expiration if not addressed; App Developer app subscribers can resubscribe up to **1 year** after expiration and recover without resubmission — [Program Renewal](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/membership/renewal/)

### Inferences
- INFERENCE — Because Apple only accepts genuine legal entities (corporation, LLC, limited partnership, etc.) for organization-type enrollment and explicitly rejects DBAs/sole-proprietorships, **forming a single-member LLC is the only path** (short of incorporating) for a solo developer who wants a business name — not their own legal name — shown publicly as the seller on the App Store.
- INFERENCE — Given the anecdotal but consistent forum evidence of slow/denied individual→organization conversions, a developer who already knows they want an org/LLC identity should enroll as an **organization from day one** rather than starting individual and converting later; conversion is explicitly support-mediated (not self-service) and lead time is unpredictable.
- INFERENCE — The org enrollment "public, functional website on an org-owned domain" requirement is a genuine early blocker: it must exist *before* enrollment verification completes, so domain registration and even a minimal placeholder site should start in parallel with (or before) D-U-N-S request.

### Gaps
- GAP — I did not find an official Apple developer.apple.com page (as opposed to Apple Support Community / Quora forum threads) directly stating the minimum age to enroll (18, "age of majority in your region") or the minor/parent-account workaround. The official enroll page confirms only "legal age of majority in your region" as a requirement, without an explicit numeric age or minor-account policy. The 13+-with-parent's-account claim is **community-sourced, not an Apple policy page**, and should be treated as unverified folklore until confirmed on an official page.
- GAP — Whether an app transfer (distinct from account-type conversion) preserves existing subscribers' active subscriptions, ratings, and reviews was not confirmed by a directly-fetched Apple primary-source paragraph in this session (only the eligibility *criteria* page was fetched; the "Overview of app transfer" page itself, which likely states this, was not fetched due to tool-budget constraints). Flag as needing direct confirmation from [Overview of app transfer](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/transfer-an-app/overview-of-app-transfer/).

**Owner / lead time / failure mode:**
- Enrollment itself (individual): **HUMAN-ONLY** (2FA'd Apple Account, legal-name entry, $99 payment) — minutes to complete, verification can add delay. Failure mode: misspelled/aliased legal name delays approval.
- Enrollment (organization): **HUMAN-ONLY** for binding-authority attestation and payment; **AGENT-CAN-DO** for drafting the placeholder org website content and D&B lookup research. Lead time: **up to 7 business days just for D-U-N-S**, plus Apple's own org-verification pass on top — budget **1–3 weeks** realistically for a brand-new organization with no existing D-U-N-S record. Failure mode: missing domain-matched email or non-functional website silently stalls verification indefinitely.
- Individual→organization conversion: **HUMAN-ONLY**, support-ticket driven, **unpredictable lead time (weeks, sometimes denied)** — failure mode: a subscription launch timeline built around "we'll convert to an LLC entity later" can be derailed for weeks with no guaranteed outcome.
- Membership lapse: **AGENT-CAN-OBSERVE** (agent can watch membership/agreement status and flag renewal deadlines); renewing is **HUMAN-ONLY** (payment). Failure mode if missed: app removed from sale (revenue stops accruing for new purchases) but existing subscribers are NOT immediately cut off — so the business-critical failure is "no new revenue," not "refund/chargeback storm."

## 4. EU Digital Services Act (DSA) trader status

### Takeaway
Every developer — even one who never intends to sell in the EU — is asked to declare **trader or non-trader status** in App Store Connect; traders must supply an address, phone, and email that Apple **publishes on the EU App Store product page**. Apps without verified trader status have already been (as of 2026-09-27) removed from the 27 EU storefronts; this is a live, present-tense enforcement state, not a future deadline.

### Cited Findings
- FACT — Legal basis: DSA Articles 30 and 31 require Apple to verify and display trader contact information for all traders distributing apps in the EU — [Manage European Union Digital Services Act trader requirements](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-compliance-information/manage-european-union-digital-services-act-trader-requirements/)
- FACT — Required info: for individuals, an **address or P.O. box**, phone number, email; for organizations, an address (auto-populated from D-U-N-S), phone, email; both must certify legal compliance of their products/services and may need to provide documentation verifying the business/legal name and address — same source.
- FACT — Published publicly on the App Store product page for apps distributed in the EU: address (or D-U-N-S-derived address), phone number, email, and optionally a Labels and Markings URL — same source.
- FACT — A non-trader declaration option exists ("This is not a trader account"); consequence: "Consumers in the EU will be informed that consumer rights stemming from applicable consumer protection laws won't apply to contracts between you and them." — same source.
- FACT — Trader status can be toggled per-app after the initial account-level declaration — same source.
- FACT (timeline) — New apps and app updates required trader info to be approved starting **October 16, 2024**; apps without verified trader status were removed from the EU App Store **February 17, 2025** — [Apps without trader status will be removed from the App Store in the EU](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=einwn76m) (Apple Developer News). As of the current date (2026-09-27) this removal is already in effect and ongoing, not an upcoming deadline.
- INFERENCE (secondary, passion.io help article — not independently confirmed on an Apple-owned page in this session) — Developers must declare a trader status even if they don't distribute in the EU at all.

### Inferences
- INFERENCE — Because the DSA-required address/phone/email is **published publicly** (unlike the tax/banking info, which stays private), a solo developer using their home address will have that home address shown to any EU visitor of their App Store page. This is a genuine privacy exposure distinct from the "seller name" issue in §3, and a registered-agent address, virtual mailbox, or (once formed) an LLC's registered address is the practical mitigation — a decision a human must make deliberately, not one an agent should default on.

### Gaps
- GAP — Could not confirm from an Apple-owned page in this session whether declaring "non-trader" has any interaction with being allowed to sell paid apps/subscriptions at all in the EU (i.e., can a non-trader still sell a subscription, just without EU consumer-protection contract terms attaching?), versus being a hobbyist/free-app-only declaration. Recommend explicit verification before assuming a solo commercial subscription developer can safely choose "non-trader."

**Owner / lead time / failure mode:** Declaring trader/non-trader status and entering the published contact info is **HUMAN-ONLY** (personal address, phone, email entered into a form whose output becomes public — this is exactly the kind of personal-data-into-a-form-with-public-consequence action that needs explicit human decision-making, not agent autofill). Lead time: the declaration itself is immediate, but must be done "next time you submit a new app" per Apple's own text — i.e., it can silently block your very first submission if not anticipated. Failure mode if skipped/incomplete: **app is rejected or removed from all 27 EU storefronts**, unrelated to any other agreement/tax/banking status — this is an independent gate from the Paid Apps Agreement.

## 5. Commission: 30%, Small Business Program, year-2 rate, 2025–2026 changes

### Takeaway
Standard commission is 30/70 (Apple/developer). A solo developer with ≤$1M in prior-year proceeds (or who is simply new) qualifies for the **App Store Small Business Program at 15%** from day one; separately, **any** developer's subscription commission drops to 15% once a given subscriber has accumulated 12 paid months in the same subscription group. Enrollment in the Small Business Program is **not retroactive** to January 1 of the year you enroll.

### Cited Findings
- FACT — Small Business Program commission: **15%** on paid apps and Apple In-App Purchases (vs. standard 30%); EU developers on the EU Alternative Terms get **10%** commission on subscriptions after a subscriber's first year — [App Store Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)
- FACT — Eligibility: developers who earned **up to $1,000,000 USD in proceeds** (sales net of Apple's commission and certain taxes/adjustments) in the prior calendar year qualify; **new developers also qualify** by default — same source.
- FACT — Exceeding $1M **during** the current year: standard 30% applies going forward only — **not retroactive** to earlier sales that same year. Falling back below $1M in a later year allows re-qualification the following year — same source.
- FACT — Enrollment effective date: the commission change takes effect **15 days after the end of the fiscal calendar month in which enrollment is approved** (Apple's own example: approved Feb 10, 2022 → effective Mar 14, 2022) — same source. This is the direct answer to "is 15% retroactive": **no**, it is prospective only, and there's a built-in ~15-day-plus lag even after approval.
- FACT — How to enroll: be the Account Holder; accept the latest Paid Apps Agreement (Schedule 2); declare all **Associated Developer Accounts**; then enroll via the program's enroll page — same source.
- FACT — Associated Developer Account definition: any account you own/control >50%, that controls you >50%, over which you have ultimate decision-making authority, or that has such authority over you. **Combined proceeds across all associated accounts must stay ≤$1M** to qualify — same source.
- FACT/well-corroborated (multiple independent secondary sources — RevenueCat, and general Apple subscription-commission documentation referenced in search results, not independently re-fetched from developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/ this session) — For **any** developer (Small Business Program or not), once a subscriber has accumulated **12 months of paid service within the same subscription group**, the developer's share rises from 70% to **85%** (i.e., Apple's commission drops from 30% to 15%) for that subscriber going forward. Free-trial time does not count toward the 12 months. If a subscription lapses and is recovered within **60 days**, the paid-service day count continues from where it left off; if recovered after 60 days, the day count resets to zero and the rate reverts to 70%/30%. — [RevenueCat: The 15% App Store Fee](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/small-business-program) (secondary write-up, flagged as such; recommend cross-checking against developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/ directly, which was not fetched in this session due to tool-budget limits).
- PARTIAL/GAP (2025–2026 changes) — Search results surfaced a **Schedule 2 and 3 agreement update dated December 17, 2025 (v126)** and an Apple-published **DMA Study (November 2025)** on how commission changes affect app prices, both of which strongly suggest EU Digital Markets Act (DMA)-driven commission/fee-structure changes (e.g., Core Technology Commission, tiered EU business terms) were active/evolving in this window. I did not have tool budget remaining to fetch these documents directly and confirm specifics. **This is a genuine, flagged gap** — the report should note that EU commission/fee terms changed materially in the 2025–2026 window and a dedicated follow-up read of [Schedule 2 and 3 (PDF, v126, 17 Dec 2025)](https://developer.apple.com/support/downloads/terms/schedules/Schedule-2-and-3-English.pdf) and the [DMA Study](https://developer.apple.com/download/files/DMA-Study-Nov-2025.pdf) is warranted before finalizing any EU-specific commission guidance.

### Inferences
- INFERENCE — For a brand-new solo developer's very first subscriber, the 15% Small Business Program rate is likely to apply from the very first sale (new developers qualify by default), making the 30%→15% "year one" framing largely moot for a true first-timer — the bigger lever is the Small Business Program enrollment step itself (an easy-to-miss opt-in, not automatic) rather than waiting a year.
- INFERENCE — Because Small Business Program enrollment requires first accepting Schedule 2, and Schedule 2 requires tax+banking to reach Active (§1–2), the practical sequence is: enroll in Program → sign Schedule 2 → complete tax/banking → *then* enroll in Small Business Program — skipping straight to Small Business Program enrollment before Schedule 2 is Active is not possible.

### Gaps
- GAP — Full detail on 2025–2026 EU DMA-driven commission/fee changes (Core Technology Commission, tiered store-services fees, "Alternative Terms" specifics) not verified in depth this session; flagged above for follow-up.
- GAP — Did not independently re-verify the 85%/15% year-2 subscription-commission mechanic against developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/ directly (relied on a well-corroborated secondary synthesis); recommend the report-writer treat this as high-confidence but not primary-source-quoted.

**Owner / lead time / failure mode:** Small Business Program enrollment — **AGENT-CAN-DO** the eligibility check and drafting of associated-account disclosures for human review, but the actual acceptance click is **HUMAN-ONLY** (Account Holder, binding declaration). Lead time: enrollment can be submitted immediately once Schedule 2 is Active, but the 15% rate doesn't take effect until **15+ days after the fiscal month of approval** — so a developer expecting 15% on their very first sale must enroll *before* that first sale, ideally in the same window as signing Schedule 2. Failure mode if missed: developer pays 30% instead of 15% on all sales until enrollment catches up — a permanent, non-recoverable loss on past sales (no retroactivity).

## 6. Payouts: fiscal calendar, timing, thresholds, realistic first-payout calendar

### Takeaway
Apple pays out **once per Apple fiscal month** (a 4-4-5-week fiscal calendar, not calendar months), roughly **33 days after that fiscal month closes**, on a Thursday, **only if** accumulated earnings in that currency meet Apple's minimum payment threshold — below-threshold balances roll forward and keep accumulating. Combined with the agreement/tax/banking gates in §1–2, a realistic first-payout timeline for a brand-new solo developer's very first subscriber is on the order of **6–10+ weeks** after that first sale, assuming the threshold is met on the first try.

### Cited Findings
- FACT (Apple's own stated requirements, synthesized from App Store Connect Help's "Overview of receiving payments" / "View payments and proceeds" pages) — To receive any payment: have an **active Paid Applications Agreement**, provide **banking information**, **exceed the minimum monthly payment threshold for that region/currency**, and fulfill any invoicing requirements — [App Store Connect Help: getting paid](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/getting-paid/overview-of-receiving-payments) (page content reflected via search synthesis; direct fetch of this exact page was not completed this session due to a tool-rate-limit, so treat as FACT-via-secondary-quotation of Apple's own text rather than a directly-read primary source).
- INFERENCE/secondary (multiple independent finance/analytics vendor write-ups — RevenueCat, Adapty, Superwall, lastapp.ai — consistent with each other but none is Apple's own page) — Apple pays **12 times per year**, once per fiscal month, about **33 days after the fiscal month closes**, landing on a **Thursday**; the gap between an individual sale and the resulting deposit is typically **5 to 9 weeks** depending where in the fiscal month the sale falls. Apple's fiscal calendar uses a **4-4-5 week** structure (13 weeks/quarter), so fiscal "months" are 28 or 35 days long and close dates shift monthly. — [RevenueCat: Apple fiscal calendar and payment dates](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/apple-fiscal-calendar-year-payment-dates), [Adapty: Apple Fiscal Calendar 2026](https://adapty.io/apple-fiscal-calendar/)
- FACT — Apple does **not** pay every fiscal month automatically; payment is issued only once accumulated earnings in a given currency **meet or exceed** Apple's minimum threshold for that currency; sub-threshold balances roll forward and accumulate — same synthesis, consistent with Apple's own described mechanism.

### Inferences
- INFERENCE (constructed from above) — A realistic calendar for a first-ever subscriber: Day 0, subscriber pays. That sale lands in whichever Apple fiscal month is open (up to ~35 days). The fiscal month then closes and Apple's ~33-day payment-processing window starts. Total elapsed time from first sale to a Thursday deposit: roughly **6 to 10 weeks**, *provided* (a) the Paid Apps Agreement was already Active, tax and banking were already complete **before** the sale (otherwise add the §1–2 delay on top), and (b) the single subscriber's payment alone clears the minimum threshold for that currency (see Gap below — for a genuinely first/only subscriber on a low-priced subscription, threshold-clearing is not guaranteed and the payout could roll forward additional months).

### Gaps
- GAP — Could not find the specific **USD minimum payment threshold dollar amount** on an Apple-owned page in this session (search results referenced "minimum payment threshold" conceptually but not a specific current USD figure). This materially affects whether a single low-price subscriber's first payment clears in the very next payable fiscal month or rolls forward for several months — flag this as a concrete open question for the report, since it directly changes the "when does money arrive" answer for the exact scenario in the objective (one subscriber, presumably a low-dollar subscription).
- GAP — Did not directly fetch developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/getting-paid/overview-of-receiving-payments in this session (rate-limited); the content above is trustworthy (search-tool-quoted) but should be spot-checked against the live page by the report-writer if precision matters.

**Owner / lead time / failure mode:** Entirely **AGENT-CAN-OBSERVE** (an agent can watch App Store Connect's Payments and Financial Reports section and report status) — no action is agent-performable here beyond monitoring, since payout is automatic once gates clear. Failure mode: a developer who assumes "sale = same-month cash" will be surprised by a 6–10-week lag; a developer whose first subscriber's payment doesn't clear the currency threshold will see **zero visible payout** for one or more additional fiscal months even though the Paid Apps Agreement, tax, and banking are all fully Active — this can look identical to "something is broken" when it is actually expected behavior.

## 7. Taxes: VAT/sales tax Apple collects, price adjustments, US income-tax basics, 1099s

### Takeaway
Apple, as merchant of record, collects and remits **end-customer** sales tax/VAT/GST on your behalf in the countries where it operates that way (confirmed explicitly by Apple for at least Mexico; broadly corroborated for "all US states and most VAT countries" by secondary sources). This is separate from the **"tax on commission" invoices** Apple issues developers for VAT/GST Apple itself pays on **its own commission** in specific countries (Canada, Japan, Malaysia, Mexico, Singapore, South Korea, Thailand). Apple does **not** issue a 1099-MISC; it **does** issue a **1099-K** to US persons/entities above a reporting threshold — and there is a live conflict between what Apple's own help page states and current IRS rules that the report should flag explicitly. Separately, a solo-proprietor developer's own US federal income tax obligations run through **Schedule C** and **Schedule SE**, with quarterly estimated payments.

### Cited Findings
- FACT (Apple-specific, per-country example) — "Apple will collect and remit the VAT directly to the tax authority in Mexico for: Non-tax registered individuals based in Mexico or outside of Mexico..." — [Manage tax information](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-tax-information/provide-tax-information/) (Apple's own tax-info page, confirming the marketplace-facilitator mechanism is real and country-specific, not a blanket global statement).
- INFERENCE/secondary (not independently re-confirmed on a single Apple page for the "blanket" framing) — "Apple collects and remits all the necessary taxes — including sales tax in all US states and value-added tax (VAT) in most other countries" — [RevenueCat: Understanding App Store Payments and Revenue Reporting](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/understanding-app-store-payments); similarly, "Apple and Google act as the merchant of record... they typically calculate, collect, and remit sales tax or VAT based on the buyer's location" — [Kruze Consulting](https://kruzeconsulting.com/) (secondary/accounting-firm write-up). Treat the *general, cross-jurisdiction* framing as INFERENCE corroborated by two independent secondary sources, resting on a FACT-confirmed mechanism (Mexico) from Apple itself.
- FACT — "Tax on commission" invoices (a **different** thing — VAT/GST Apple pays on *its own* commission fee, invoiced to the developer for input-tax-credit purposes, not sales tax collected from customers) are issued for developers based in: Canada, Japan, Malaysia, Mexico, Singapore, South Korea, Thailand — plus self-billing invoices for Australia/Ireland-based developers with valid ABN/VAT IDs, and Brazil annual/monthly withholding-tax certificates — [Manage invoices and other tax documents](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-tax-information/manage-invoices-and-other-tax-documents/). This distinction matters: a US solo developer selling only in the US will not see any of these commission-tax documents at all — that is separate from, and not a substitute for, understanding that Apple collects US sales tax from the *customer* on the sale itself.
- FACT — Apple does **not** issue a US **1099-MISC**: "Sales on the App Store are between you and the customer. You're the seller of copyrighted works, and we make payments to you for products or goods, which are specifically exempt from reporting on Form 1099-MISC." — [Manage invoices and other tax documents](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-tax-information/manage-invoices-and-other-tax-documents/)
- FACT — Apple **does** issue a **1099-K** to US-based individuals/entities meeting the threshold, mailed **by January 31** of the following year. Apple's own help page (as read 2026-09-27) states the threshold as: **unadjusted gross sales of at least $5,000** for the calendar year (with a footnote that "prior to calendar year 2024" the threshold was $20,000 gross AND more than 200 transactions) — same source.
- **CONFLICT FLAGGED** — Apple's stated $5,000 threshold (above, as currently published on developer.apple.com) appears to be **stale relative to current federal law**. Multiple 2026 IRS sources state the federal 1099-K threshold was **reverted back to the pre-2024 standard of over $20,000 AND more than 200 transactions**, effective for tax year 2025 onward, under the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" (reversing the phased-down $5,000/$2,500/$600 thresholds that had previously been scheduled): "third party settlement organizations are not required to file Forms 1099-K unless the gross amount of reportable payment transactions to a payee exceeds $20,000 [and 200 transactions]" — [IRS: FAQs on Form 1099-K threshold under the One Big Beautiful Bill](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom) and [IRS: Understanding your Form 1099-K](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/understanding-your-form-1099-k) (both irs.gov, checked 2026-09-27 via search). **This is an explicit, unresolved conflict between Apple's own published developer help content and current IRS guidance** — the report-writer should note that regardless of which threshold controls whether Apple *issues* a 1099-K, **the underlying income is taxable and must be self-reported by the developer independent of any 1099-K threshold** (this is the practically important point for a solo developer, since a small first-subscriber's revenue is very unlikely to trigger a 1099-K under either threshold, but is still fully taxable).
- FACT (US self-employment tax basics) — A sole proprietor/independent contractor is "self-employed" per the IRS; must generally file an annual return and pay **estimated taxes quarterly** (Form 1040-ES); must pay **self-employment tax** (Social Security + Medicare) via **Schedule SE**, computed from net profit/loss reported on **Schedule C** (Form 1040); must file a return if net self-employment earnings are **$400 or more**, even below the standard filing threshold — [IRS: Self-employed individuals tax center](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employed-individuals-tax-center) (checked 2026-09-27).

### Inferences
- INFERENCE — A first-time solo developer's first subscriber's revenue will almost certainly be far below either the $5,000 or $20,000 1099-K thresholds, meaning **no 1099-K will likely be issued for a long time** — but the developer must still track and self-report this income on Schedule C from the very first dollar (the $400 net-earnings filing threshold is what actually governs the developer's own obligation, not Apple's 1099-K threshold).
- INFERENCE — Because Apple collects/remits end-customer sales tax/VAT itself in most jurisdictions, a US solo developer generally does **not** need to separately register for state sales tax collection on App Store subscription sales specifically — but this does not necessarily exempt the developer from state **economic nexus** reporting obligations more broadly (a secondary source, Afternoon.co, raised this nuance) and is exactly the kind of point that needs a tax professional, not a research note, to finalize for any given state.

### Gaps
- GAP — Did not find, and flag as needing professional/direct-primary confirmation: Apple's specific "automatic price adjustment" mechanics for subscriptions (i.e., Apple auto-adjusting local-currency subscription prices in response to tax-rate or FX changes) — this is referenced in Apple's pricing/subscription documentation generally but was not directly fetched this session; recommend the report-writer or a follow-up check [Manage pricing for auto-renewable subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/manage-pricing-for-auto-renewable-subscriptions/) directly.
- GAP — The Apple-vs-IRS 1099-K threshold conflict above is unresolved; it's possible Apple's help page is simply not yet updated post-OBBBA, or that Apple chooses to report at a lower voluntary threshold than the federal minimum requires — I could not determine which from the sources gathered.

**Owner / lead time / failure mode:** Sales-tax/VAT collection — **AGENT-CAN-OBSERVE only** (fully automatic, Apple-side; nothing for the developer or an agent to configure for a standard App Store subscription). US income-tax filing (Schedule C/SE, quarterly estimates) — **HUMAN-ONLY**, ideally with a CPA; **flag explicitly: this is a professional-services point, not a DIY checklist item**, especially the quarterly-estimated-payment cadence which has its own penalty regime if missed. Failure mode if the developer ignores quarterly estimated taxes: IRS underpayment penalties accrue quietly over the year regardless of how small the App Store income is.

## 8. Sole proprietor vs single-member LLC, EIN, DBA/business license

### Takeaway
Apple does not care whether you're a sole proprietor or an LLC for the *individual* enrollment track — a sole proprietorship is **not** a distinct legal entity in Apple's eyes and **must** enroll as an "individual," under your personal legal name, regardless of any DBA/EIN you obtain. Forming a genuine single-member LLC (or corporation) is the **only** way to unlock Apple's "organization" enrollment track and have a business name — not your personal name — shown as the public seller. This is a real, Apple-relevant reason a solo developer might form an LLC even before it matters for liability.

### Cited Findings
- FACT — "If your legal status is a sole proprietorship/single person business, enroll as an individual" — you cannot use a DBA/EIN to appear as an organization; Apple explicitly rejects DBAs, fictitious businesses, trade names, and branches for org-type enrollment, and a D-U-N-S lookup returning "sole proprietorship" status will block organization enrollment — [D-U-N-S Number](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/membership/D-U-N-S/)
- FACT — Legal entity types Apple *does* accept for organization enrollment: "corporation, limited partnership, or limited liability company" — same source. An LLC is explicitly on this list; a bare sole proprietorship is not.
- FACT (general US tax-structure reference, not Apple-specific) — The IRS's own "Business structures" guidance describes sole proprietorship, partnership, corporation, S corporation, and LLC ("a relatively new business structure allowed by state statute") as the common forms, each governing which return form is filed — [IRS: Self-employed individuals tax center](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employed-individuals-tax-center) (checked 2026-09-27). The IRS page does not itself elaborate the sole-prop-vs-LLC liability distinction on this particular page; that is general, well-established US business law (a single-member LLC creates a liability shield separating personal assets from business debts/claims, which a sole proprietorship does not) rather than something sourced fresh in this session — **flag for professional (attorney) confirmation for the developer's specific state and situation**, not a substitute for legal advice.

### Inferences
- INFERENCE — For the specific purpose of hiding a personal legal name from public App Store listings (both the "seller name" per §3 and the DSA-published address/phone/email per §4), forming a single-member LLC is the practical lever: it (a) satisfies Apple's "legal entity" bar for org enrollment (unlocking a business seller name), and (b) can supply a business address (e.g., a registered-agent or LLC-registered address) instead of a home address for the DSA trader disclosure. Neither of these is achievable by simply obtaining an EIN or filing a DBA while remaining a sole proprietor, because Apple's own D-U-N-S/legal-entity check will still classify the underlying business as a sole proprietorship.
- INFERENCE (unsourced this session, standard/free process per general knowledge, not independently re-verified via a direct IRS.gov EIN-application fetch) — A US-based individual can typically obtain an EIN directly from the IRS online, free of charge, essentially the same day — this is commonly known but was **not** independently confirmed by fetching irs.gov's EIN application page in this session; flag as GAP.

### Gaps
- GAP — No LLC-specific state-filing-fee figures were sourced this session (fees vary by state, commonly cited as ranging from $0 to several hundred dollars, but I did not fetch SBA.gov or a specific state's Secretary of State page to confirm current 2026 figures). The final report/table should either source this per-state or clearly present it as a wide, unsourced-this-session range requiring the developer to check their own state.
- GAP — Did not source whether a single-member LLC's default federal tax treatment (disregarded entity, still filing Schedule C, unless it elects corporate taxation) was independently confirmed via a fetched IRS page this session — this is standard, well-established federal tax law, but the specific citation was not pulled in this research pass; recommend the report flag this as "standard IRS treatment, verify current form/publication reference before publishing as a citation."
- GAP — Local business license / DBA requirements are county/city-specific in the US and were not researched at that granularity this session; note only that **Apple does not require or recognize a DBA at all** for its own purposes (per §3/§8 findings above), so any DBA/local license a developer obtains is solely for reasons unrelated to the App Store (e.g., opening a business bank account under a trade name, local compliance) — this itself is worth stating plainly in the report so a developer doesn't assume a DBA helps with Apple.

**Owner / lead time / failure mode:** Forming an LLC — **HUMAN-ONLY**, typically via a state filing (self-service or via a formation service/attorney); **flag explicitly that a solo developer should consult a CPA/attorney** for the liability and tax-election trade-offs, which are genuinely state- and situation-specific and outside what any generic checklist can respons

ibly resolve. Lead time: state-dependent, commonly same-day to a few weeks for state approval, **before** the D-U-N-S request (§3) can even begin, which itself adds up to 7 more business days — so LLC formation is a genuine **long-lead item** if the developer wants an organization-type Apple account from day one. Failure mode if sequenced late: a developer who starts as an "individual" and later forms an LLC faces the slow/uncertain individual→organization conversion process documented in §3, potentially with weeks of delay or an outright denial requiring a fresh organization enrollment instead.
