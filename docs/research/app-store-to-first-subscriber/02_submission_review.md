## 2. First-subscription submission gotcha ("Ready to Submit" / Missing Metadata / Developer Action Needed)

### Takeaway
Apple hard-requires that the *first* auto-renewable subscription (and, separately, its subscription group) be attached to and submitted together with a brand-new app version — you cannot submit an IAP/subscription on its own the very first time. Before it can even be submitted, App Store Connect gates it behind a "Ready to Submit" status that requires a review screenshot, localized display name + description, price/availability, and (for the very first one in a group) subscription-group localization; anything short of that shows as "Missing Metadata" / "Developer Action Needed" — a to-do-list state, not a rejection. FACT, sourced primarily from Apple's own App Store Connect Help.

### Cited Findings
- "For the first In-App Purchase or subscription on an app, and any time you add a new type of product, Apple requires it to be attached to and submitted together with a new app version." Once App Review approves one or more IAPs/subscriptions, further products of an already-approved type can be submitted without a new app version — [Apple Developer Help: Submit an in-app purchase](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-submissions-to-app-review/submit-an-in-app-purchase) (checked 2026-09-27).
- When submitting a new subscription, it must be submitted together with its **subscription group**; each new subscription group must be submitted with at least one of its subscriptions — same source.
- Submission workflow: in App Store Connect, open the app version, scroll to "In-App Purchases and Subscriptions," click "Select In-App Purchases or Subscriptions" (or "Edit"), choose the items, click Done, then submit the version for review with the items attached; up to 200 IAP/subscription items can ride in one submission — same source.
- In-App Purchases with **content hosting can no longer be submitted for review**, and IAP/subscriptions are **not supported on watchOS** — same source.
- Guideline 2.1(b) (App Completeness), the App Review Guidelines primary text: "If you offer in-app purchases in your app, make sure they are complete, up-to-date, visible to the reviewer and functional. If any configured in-app purchase items cannot be found or reviewed in your app, explain the reason in your review notes." — [Apple: App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (fetched live 2026-09-27, page states "Last Updated: June 8, 2026").
- "Developer Action Required" on an IAP/subscription = "Missing Metadata": commonly a missing review screenshot, missing localized display name/description, or an inactive/expired Paid Applications Agreement; it is a checklist state meaning the product isn't finished being configured, not a review rejection — [PTKD Journal: 'Developer Action Required' on App Store Subscriptions & IAP](https://ptkd.com/journal/developer-action-required-in-app-purchases-app-store) (secondary/practitioner source, checked 2026-09-27); consistent with [Apple Help: In-App Purchase statuses reference](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/in-app-purchases-and-subscriptions/in-app-purchase-statuses/) (title/reference located, not fully fetched — see Gaps).
- A specific common trap: **subscription-group localization** (display name for the group itself, at the bottom of the subscription-group page in App Store Connect) is separate from each individual subscription's own localization — if the group localization is skipped, every subscription plan inside it stays permanently "Missing Metadata" regardless of the individual plan's fields being complete — [codegenes.net: How to Fix In-App Purchases Stuck in 'Missing Metadata' State](https://www.codegenes.net/blog/in-app-purchases-stuck-in-missing-metadata-state/) (secondary, checked 2026-09-27).
- You cannot sell or finish configuring in-app purchases at all without an **active Paid Applications Agreement** (signed, with current tax forms and banking details) in App Store Connect's Business/Agreements section — Apple periodically updates this agreement, and an outstanding un-accepted update silently pauses monetization even for previously-working IAPs — [PTKD Journal](https://ptkd.com/journal/developer-action-required-in-app-purchases-app-store) (secondary, checked 2026-09-27).
- Practitioner guidance frames the widely-seen Guideline 2.1 rejection message "we were unable to locate/could not find your in-app purchase" as almost always a *review-notes/config* problem — reviewer can't find the paywall entry point, the IAP isn't in "Ready to Submit"/hasn't been attached to the version, sandbox/demo credentials weren't provided, or the paywall is gated behind onboarding/geo/server flags the reviewer can't clear — [SaaSCity: App Store Connect 'In-App Purchases and Subscriptions' Section Missing? Guideline 2.1 IAP fix (2026)](https://saascity.io/blog/app-store-connect-in-app-purchases-subscriptions-section-missing-fix-2026) (secondary/practitioner, 2026, checked 2026-09-27).

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: For a genuinely first-time solo-developer subscription app, the safest sequencing is: (1) fully configure the subscription group + all planned subscription tiers and get every one to "Ready to Submit" (screenshot, localized name/description, group localization, pricing, availability) *before* touching the version submission; (2) attach all of them to the same first version submission so they enter review together; (3) write explicit Review Notes describing exactly where the paywall is in the UI and provide a working demo account, since first-time reviewers have no prior context for the app.
- INFERENCE: Because subsequent (non-first) IAPs of an already-approved type don't need a new app version, a solo dev who anticipates adding tiers later should get at least one subscription of each intended "type" (e.g., one auto-renewable) approved as early as possible to unlock faster, version-independent iteration afterward.

## 3. Most common first-time-subscription-app rejections (guideline numbers, primary text, and 2026 gotchas)

### Takeaway
The App Review Guidelines page (developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/, primary source, "Last Updated: June 8, 2026") gives exact, current wording for every cited guideline; the highest-frequency first-timer failure modes are 2.1 (incomplete/unfindable IAP, no demo account), 3.1.2 paywall-disclosure and "dark pattern" issues (an active 2026 enforcement crackdown per secondary sources), 5.1.1(v)/5.1.2 privacy items (account deletion; and a **new 2025-2026 requirement to disclose sharing personal data with third-party AI**), and 4.2/4.3 (minimum functionality / template-app spam) for thin apps. Review outcomes are either "Rejected" (Resolution Center message, fixable and resubmittable) or "Metadata Rejected" (only listing/metadata is wrong, binary is fine, no new build needed); there's also a distinct post-decision Appeal path (App Review Board) separate from ordinary Resolution Center back-and-forth.

### Cited Findings
**Guideline 2.1 App Completeness** (verbatim from primary source):
- 2.1(a): "Submissions to App Review... should be final versions with all necessary metadata and fully functional URLs included; placeholder text, empty websites, and other temporary content should be scrubbed... include demo account info (and turn on your back-end service!) if your app includes a login. If you are unable to provide a demo account due to legal or security obligations, you may include a built-in demo mode in lieu of a demo account with prior approval by Apple... We will reject incomplete app bundles and binaries that crash or exhibit obvious technical problems." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (fetched 2026-09-27).
- 2.1(b): "If you offer in-app purchases in your app, make sure they are complete, up-to-date, visible to the reviewer and functional. If any configured in-app purchase items cannot be found or reviewed in your app, explain the reason in your review notes." — same source. This is the exact textual basis for the classic "could not find your in-app purchase" rejection.

**Guideline 2.3 Accurate Metadata** (verbatim, selected sub-items relevant to subscription apps):
- 2.3.2: app description/screenshots/previews must clearly indicate which featured items/subscriptions require additional purchase; if promoting IAPs on the Store, the IAP Display Name/Screenshot/Description must be public-audience-appropriate and the app must properly handle `SKPaymentTransactionObserver` so purchases complete seamlessly on launch.
- 2.3.6: age-rating questionnaire must be answered honestly; mis-rating can trigger a regulatory inquiry.
- 2.3.7: app name limited to **30 characters**; no keyword-stuffing with trademarks/competitor names/pricing in metadata.
- 2.3.8: all metadata/icons/screenshots (including IAP icons) must be appropriate for a 4+ rating regardless of the app's actual age rating.
— [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (fetched 2026-09-27).

**Guideline 2.5.1** (verbatim): "Apps may only use public APIs and must run on the currently shipping OS... Keep your apps up-to-date and make sure you phase out any deprecated features, frameworks or technologies that will no longer be supported... Apps should use APIs and frameworks for their intended purposes and indicate that integration in their app description." — same source. INFERENCE: this is the guideline most likely to bite a solo dev who ships a build made with beta/mismatched SDKs or private APIs (see Mac App Store section, Q9).

**Guideline 3.1.1 In-App Purchase** (verbatim, key lines): apps unlocking features/functionality (subscriptions, in-game currency, premium content, full-version unlock) "must use in-app purchase... may not use their own mechanisms to unlock content or functionality, such as license keys, augmented reality markers, QR codes, cryptocurrencies..." Restorable IAPs need a restore mechanism; credits/in-game currencies "may not expire." — same source.

**Guideline 3.1.2 Subscriptions** (verbatim, key lines):
- 3.1.2(a) Permissible uses: "If you offer an auto-renewable subscription, you must provide **ongoing value** to the customer, and the subscription period must last **at least seven days** and be available across all of the user's devices." Lists acceptable value categories (new levels, episodic content, multiplayer, substantive updates, large/continually-updated media libraries, SaaS, cloud support). Also: subscriptions must not require extra tasks (social posts, contact uploads, check-ins) beyond payment to get what was paid for; can't strip existing paid functionality when converting an app to subscription-based; "Apps that attempt to scam users... trick users into purchasing a subscription under false pretenses or engage in bait-and-switch... will be removed."
- 3.1.2(b) Upgrades/Downgrades: must be seamless; users must not be able to inadvertently double-subscribe to variants of the same thing.
- 3.1.2(c) Subscription Information: "Before asking a customer to subscribe, you should clearly describe what the user will get for the price... Ensure you clearly communicate the requirements described in **Schedule 2 of the Apple Developer Program License Agreement**."
— [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (fetched 2026-09-27).
- **2026 "paywall dark pattern" enforcement wave (secondary sources, not primary guideline text change but an enforcement trend)**: multiple 2026-dated practitioner posts describe Apple issuing a wave of identical Guideline 3.1.2 rejections against "toggle paywalls" (pre-checked/pre-toggled plan selection defaults, especially defaulting to a paid tier) starting roughly Q1 2026 — [Adapty: "Your toggle paywall is about to get rejected"](https://adapty.io) dated 2026-02-11 (via search snippet, not independently fetched — see Gaps); [RevenueFlo: "Common iOS Paywall Rejections And the Fixes That Work"](https://revenueflo.com/blog/common-ios-paywall-rejections-and-the-fixes-that-work), dated 2026-02-11 (snippet only); [Volpis: "Common App Store Rejection Reasons"](https://volpis.com/blog/app-store-rejection-reasons), dated 2026-04-28 (snippet only, coincidentally same date as the SDK-26 enforcement deadline). These are SECONDARY/practitioner sources surfaced via a Google search snippet (not independently opened/verified page-by-page) — treat the specific "toggle paywall" mechanism-of-rejection as INFERENCE-strength, not confirmed primary-source FACT, though the underlying Guideline 3.1.2(c) requirement to "clearly describe what the user will get for the price" before asking them to subscribe is primary-sourced.
- Practitioner consensus (multiple secondary sources, e.g. RevenueCat's "ultimate guide to App Store rejections") on recurring paywall-review pain points for 3.1.2: price and billing period must be clearly, unambiguously visible on the paywall screen itself (not just buried in a linked terms page) before the user commits to purchase; free-trial-to-paid transition terms must be disclosed; and the paywall must be reachable/dismissible so a reviewer can test the flow without being forced to pay — [RevenueCat: The ultimate guide to App Store rejections](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/) (secondary, checked 2026-09-27 via search).

**Guideline 3.1.2 required legal links — Terms of Use (EULA) and Privacy Policy, both in-app AND in metadata**: Apple's Schedule 2 requirement (referenced directly by 3.1.2(c)) and Guideline 5.1.1(i) together mean a subscription app needs: (1) a Privacy Policy URL in the App Store Connect metadata field, (2) the same privacy policy linked and easily accessible **inside the app** (5.1.1(i), verbatim: "All apps must include a link to their privacy policy in the App Store Connect metadata field **and within the app** in an easily accessible manner"), and (3) practitioner sources uniformly report that Apple also expects a Terms of Use/EULA link both on the paywall/purchase screen and in the App Store Connect "Agreements, Business, and Legal" > App Description area — [Reddit r/iOSProgramming search-snippet consensus: "Your app has to have a way to access TOS and privacy policy... place them in at least their paywall"] (secondary/community, checked 2026-09-27 — see Gaps for primary EULA-placement citation).

**Guideline 4.2 Minimum Functionality** (verbatim, key lines): "Your app should include features, content, and UI that elevate it beyond a repackaged website. If your app is not particularly useful, unique, or 'app-like,' it doesn't belong on the App Store." 4.2.6: "Apps created from a commercialized template or app generation service will be rejected unless submitted directly by the provider of the app's content" — directly relevant to any solo dev shipping a templated/boilerplate-generated app. 4.2.2: apps "shouldn't primarily be marketing materials, advertisements, web clippings, content aggregators, or a collection of links." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (fetched 2026-09-27).

**Guideline 4.3 Spam** (verbatim, key lines): (a) don't create multiple Bundle IDs of essentially the same app instead of one app with IAP-based variants; (b) "Don't submit apps that are indistinguishable from what's already widely available... Certain kinds of apps, such as dating, flashlight, sound effects, wallpaper, simple timers, and fortune telling... we will not accept new submissions unless they offer a meaningfully different or improved experience." Repeated low-effort submissions "may lead to removal from the Apple Developer Program." — same source. Directly relevant risk for a first-timer shipping a commodity-category subscription app (e.g. a generic habit tracker, journal, or "AI wrapper" utility).

**Guideline 4.8 Login Services** (verbatim): "Apps that use a third-party or social login service (such as Facebook Login, Google Sign-In, Log in with X, Sign In with LinkedIn, Login with Amazon, or WeChat Login) to set up or authenticate the user's primary account with the app must also offer as an equivalent option another [login method]..." — same source (text truncated in extraction at "another" but the rule is Apple's well-known "Sign in with Apple must be offered if any third-party social login is offered" requirement).

**Guideline 5.1.1 Data Collection and Storage** (verbatim, key sub-items):
- (i) Privacy Policies: link required in App Store Connect metadata **and** within the app; must identify what data is collected/how/why, confirm third parties (analytics, ad networks, SDKs, affiliates) provide equal protection, and explain retention/deletion policy and how a user can revoke consent or request deletion.
- (ii) Permission: consent required even for "anonymous" data; paid functionality must not be gated behind granting data access; must provide an accessible way to withdraw consent.
- (v) Account Sign-In / **Account Deletion**, verbatim: "If your app supports account creation, **you must also offer account deletion within the app**." Also: apps must let people use them without login if there's no significant account-based feature; can't require personal info beyond what's core to functionality or legally required.
— [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (fetched 2026-09-27).

**Guideline 5.1.2 Data Use and Sharing — 2025/2026 third-party-AI disclosure addition** (verbatim, primary source, confirmed live on the page dated "Last Updated: June 8, 2026"): "(i) Unless otherwise permitted by law, you may not use, transmit, or share someone's personal data without first obtaining their permission... You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, **including with third-party AI**, and obtain explicit permission before doing so. Data collected from apps may only be shared with third parties to improve the app or serve advertising... You must receive explicit permission from users via the App Tracking Transparency APIs to track their activity... Your app may not require users to enable system functionalities (e.g. push notifications, location services, tracking) in order to access functionality, content, use the app, or receive monetary or other compensation..." This is a **directly relevant, confirmed-live 2025/2026-era update**: any subscription app whose backend calls an LLM/AI API with user-entered personal data (chat content, photos, health data, etc.) must now disclose that third-party-AI sharing and get explicit permission, distinct from general privacy-policy disclosure. — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (fetched live 2026-09-27).

**Guideline 5.2 Intellectual Property** (verbatim): 5.2.1 Generally — no unauthorized trademarks/copyrighted works/patented ideas, no misleading/copycat names or metadata; app must be submitted by the entity that owns/licenses the relevant IP. 5.2.5 Apple Products — can't look confusingly similar to an Apple product/interface, can't use Apple emoji in apps/extensions/keyboards/stickers. — same source. Directly relevant to the Mac App Store case study in Q9 (mascots extracted from other vendors' apps).

**Resolution Center / Rejected vs Metadata Rejected / Appeals / Expedited review** (verbatim from primary source's "After You Submit" section, page states "Last Updated: June 8, 2026"):
- Timing: "App Review will examine your app as soon as we can. However, if your app is complex or presents new issues, it may require greater scrutiny... if your app is repeatedly rejected for the same guideline violation or you've attempted to manipulate the review process, review of your app will take longer."
- Expedite Requests: "If you have a critical timing issue, you can request an expedited review. Please respect your fellow developers by seeking expedited review only when you truly need it. If we find you're abusing this system, we may reject your requests going forward."
- Rejections: "If your app has been rejected and you have questions or would like to provide additional information, please use App Store Connect to communicate directly with the App Review team."
- Appeals: "If you disagree with the outcome of your review, please submit an appeal... You may also suggest changes to the guidelines themselves."
- Bug Fix Submissions carve-out: for apps already live, "bug fixes will not be delayed over guideline violations except for those related to legal or safety issues" if the developer explicitly invokes this process via App Store Connect messaging to the review team.
— [App Review Guidelines, "After You Submit" section](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (fetched live 2026-09-27).
- "Metadata Rejected" vs "Rejected" as distinct App Store Connect statuses (not verbatim-quoted from the guidelines page above, which doesn't name these exact status strings) is a widely-documented App Store Connect UI distinction: a binary-level "Rejected" status means the build/functionality failed review and typically needs a code fix and new build; "Metadata Rejected" means only text/screenshots/pricing/etc. failed and can be corrected and resubmitted **without uploading a new build** — this exact distinction could not be located on a primary Apple Help page in this pass (see Gaps) though it is extremely widely reported by developers and tooling docs (Despia, SaaSCity sources above).

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: For a first-time solo subscription-app developer, the single highest-leverage defensive move against 2.1/3.1.2 rejections is (a) writing detailed App Review Notes that state exactly where the paywall is, what a demo account can see, and why any IAP might not be immediately "found" (e.g., gated behind onboarding), and (b) ensuring the paywall screen itself — not just a linked page — shows price, billing period, auto-renewal disclosure, and a working restore/cancel path, since 2026 enforcement snippets suggest Apple is actively re-scrutinizing paywall UX patterns (toggle defaults, pre-selected paid tiers) under 3.1.2 even though the underlying guideline text hasn't obviously changed.
- INFERENCE: The 5.1.2(i) "third-party AI" disclosure clause is new/newly-emphasized relative to older versions of the guidelines (which historically referenced "third parties" generally but not "third-party AI" by name) — any app whose subscription value proposition involves sending user content to an LLM API (OpenAI, Anthropic, etc.) should treat this as a first-class privacy-policy and App Privacy "Nutrition Label" disclosure item, not an afterthought, given it's called out by name in the current live guidelines text.

## 5. App record fields, naming, SKU, immutability

### Takeaway
Apple's own App Information reference page gives the authoritative, current field-by-field rules: app Name is 2–30 characters and editable until submission (and again between versions); SKU and Bundle ID are both permanently locked once set (SKU at app-record creation, Bundle ID at first build upload); Primary Language can be changed at any time; and a "Made for Kids" designation becomes permanent (all future updates must follow Kids-category rules) once approved.

### Cited Findings
- **Name**: "The name must be at least two characters and no more than 30 characters. You can edit it until you submit the app to App Review. Later, you can change the name when you create a new version or the status of the app version permits editing this property." — [Apple Developer Help: App information reference](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/app-information/) (fetched live 2026-09-27). Cross-reference Guideline 2.3.7 (Q3): names are also policed for trademark/keyword-stuffing abuse and uniqueness/anti-squatting intent at review time, not just length.
- **Subtitle**: max 30 characters — same source.
- **Bundle ID**: "A unique identifier for your app that is used throughout the system... You can't change this property after you upload a build. Important: The Bundle ID property must match the bundle ID you set in the Xcode project." — same source. This is the canonical "immutable field" gotcha: the very first build upload permanently locks the app record to that bundle ID.
- **SKU**: "A unique ID you give to your app for internal tracking that's not visible to customers. The SKU can contain letters, numbers, hyphens, periods, and underscores but not start with a hyphen, period, or underscore. You can't change the SKU after you add the app to your account." — same source.
- **Apple ID** (the App Store Connect numeric app identifier, distinct from a developer's personal Apple ID): auto-generated when the app record is created, used in the App Store's desktop URL, and cannot be edited — same source.
- **Primary Language**: "The default language for the metadata... You can change the primary language at any time." — same source. This is the one core app-record field that is NOT locked.
- **Made for Kids**: "Once an app marked as Made for Kids is approved by App Review, this selection cannot be changed and all subsequent updates will need to follow the Kids category guidelines." — same source. A permanent, one-way door distinct from age rating itself.
- **Content Rights** and **License Agreement** (Apple's standard EULA applies by default across all regions unless a custom one is supplied to supersede it for specific regions) — same source.

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: Because Bundle ID lock-in happens at *first build upload* (not at app-record creation), a solo developer has a brief window — after creating the app record but before the first archive upload — where the bundle ID can still be corrected; this window closes the moment any build (even an internal-TestFlight-only one) is uploaded, so bundle-ID typos/naming-scheme mistakes should be caught in Xcode project settings before that first `xcodebuild`/Xcode Organizer upload, not after.
- INFERENCE: Given SKU is "internal tracking, not visible to customers" but permanently locked, there's no user-facing risk to getting the SKU "wrong," but it's still worth a stable, greppable convention (e.g., matching the bundle ID) since it can't be fixed later and will appear in Sales/Financial reports indefinitely.

## 8. Release: manual/automatic, phased, pre-orders, territories, time to searchable

### Takeaway
App Store Connect offers three release modes per version (manual "release yourself," fully automatic on approval, or automatic-but-not-before-a-set-date), plus an optional 7-day phased rollout (1%→2%→5%→10%→20%→50%→100% of automatic-update-eligible users) available only on version *updates*, not first submissions. Manually-released versions can take up to 24 hours to appear on the Store after release is triggered, and an app left in "Pending Developer Release" for 30+ days triggers an Apple reminder email.

### Cited Findings
- Three release options, verbatim: "Release the app yourself" (manual), "Automatically release the app after approval," and "Automatically release the app but no earlier than a specified date" (developer supplies a date/time). If setting up a **pre-order** in a country/region where the app isn't yet released, the release-option UI is dimmed and manual release is force-selected. — [Apple Developer Help: Select an App Store version release option](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-your-apps-availability/select-an-app-store-version-release-option/) (fetched live 2026-09-27).
- Manual release mechanics: after approval, status becomes **Pending Developer Release**; developer clicks "Release This Version" and confirms; **Apple emails a reminder if an app sits in Pending Developer Release for more than 30 days**; after triggering release, "it may take up to 24 hours for it to appear on the App Store"; release can be cancelled via "Cancel This Release" before it goes live. Releasing is **per-platform** — each platform version (iOS, macOS, etc.) in Pending Developer Release must be released individually. — same source.
- Manually releasing also **triggers pre-order fulfillment** in any country/region where pre-order was enabled, and makes the app available/purchasable in any region marked Available or Available on App Release — same source.
- **Phased release** (verbatim mechanics): available only when submitting a **version update** (app statuses: Prepare for Submission, Waiting for Review, In Review, Waiting for Export Compliance, Pending Developer Release, Developer Rejected, Rejected, Metadata Rejected). Rollout schedule over 7 days: Day 1 = 1%, Day 2 = 2%, Day 3 = 5%, Day 4 = 10%, Day 5 = 20%, Day 6 = 50%, Day 7 = 100%, applied only to users with **automatic updates** enabled on eligible devices, with no notification to those users that they're in the phased cohort. Anyone can still manually download the full update from the Store at any time regardless of rollout percentage. Pausable for up to 30 days total (cumulative across multiple pauses), and resuming continues from the day it was paused at. **If the app is removed from sale for any reason — including a lapsed Apple Developer Program membership — phased release stops permanently for that version** and cannot be resumed for it; a brand-new version submission is needed to restart a controlled rollout. — [Apple Developer Help: Release a version update in phases](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/update-your-app/release-a-version-update-in-phases) (fetched live 2026-09-27). Confirms the "no rollback" characteristic already flagged as covered by the coordinator (phased release only ever moves forward or pauses — it cannot revert already-updated users to the prior version).
- Territories/availability: an app's per-country/region "Available"/"Available on App Release" status (set separately in App Store Connect's Pricing and Availability section) governs where a manual release actually goes live, layered on top of the release-option and phased-release mechanics above — same source (cross-referenced from the "Note" in the manual-release section).

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: Because phased release is unavailable for an app's very first version (it only appears as an option on version *updates* to an already-shipped app), a first-time solo developer's initial launch is inherently "instant/automatic-or-manual, 0-to-100%" with no way to soft-launch a percentage of users on day one via this native mechanism — any true staged-first-launch strategy would have to rely on staggering **territory availability** (releasing to a small country/region set first, then expanding) rather than phased release.
- INFERENCE: "Time until searchable" was not independently found as a numeric primary-source claim (e.g., "X hours after release the app appears in Search") in this pass; the only numeric timing facts found are the up-to-24-hour propagation window for a manually-triggered release to appear on storefronts. Search-index-specific delay (beyond simple storefront listing) should be treated as UNKNOWN / not found rather than assumed equal to the 24-hour listing-propagation figure.

### Gaps
- Did not find a primary source specifically for pre-order mechanics beyond the passing mention above (e.g., maximum pre-order window length, up-front pricing lock rules) — should be checked against developer.apple.com/help pages on pre-orders directly if needed for the final report.

## 9. Mac App Store constraints, including the menu-bar Touch-Bar/mascot case study

### Takeaway
Multiple independent, hard technical and policy gates make the described app (private DFRFoundation/NSTouchBar calls, reading other apps' bundles/logs, Accessibility-driven window tiling of other apps, and UI assets extracted from Anthropic's and OpenAI's installed apps) **incompatible with Mac App Store distribution as designed**: App Sandbox is mandatory for the Mac App Store (Guideline 2.5.2 / Apple's own App Sandbox documentation), Apple's own DTS engineers confirm the Accessibility API does not function in a sandboxed process at all (so window tiling of other apps cannot work sandboxed), reading other apps' bundles/logs violates the sandbox's per-app-container isolation, private APIs like DFRFoundation violate Guideline 2.5.1, and using extracted third-party (Anthropic/OpenAI) mascot art without permission implicates Guideline 5.2.1 IP rules. The only realistic distribution path for this app is outside the Mac App Store, via Developer ID signing + notarization + hardened runtime, which itself has a specific, enumerated checklist of requirements.

### Cited Findings
- **App Sandbox is mandatory for Mac App Store distribution**: "To distribute a macOS app through the Mac App Store, you must enable the App Sandbox capability." — [Apple Developer Documentation: App Sandbox](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/security/app-sandbox) (fetched live 2026-09-27).
- **Accessibility API is fundamentally incompatible with the sandbox** — confirmed directly by an Apple DTS (Developer Technical Support) engineer responding to a developer whose window-manager app submission was rejected with automated-checker error **90296 "App sandbox not enabled"**: "our documentation makes it clear that the Accessibility APIs are not supported in sandboxed apps. See the 'Review functionality that is incompatible with App Sandbox' section of Protecting user data with App Sandbox." The same thread documents that `AXUIElementSetAttributeValue` (the exact API needed to reposition/tile another app's windows) "does not function correctly within the App Sandbox, even when the user has granted Accessibility permission," and Apple offers no exemption/entitlement for this — the DTS engineer explicitly declined to comment on whether any competing app on the Store is "grandfathered." — [Apple Developer Forums thread 836644, "Mac App Store submission rejected for missing sandbox — but similar apps on the store don't use it,"](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/836644) posted/answered July 2026 (fetched live 2026-09-27). This is a **direct, near-exact analog** to the case study's "tiling their windows via Accessibility" behavior — it is blocked at the automated-checker stage before even reaching human App Review.
- **Guideline 2.5.2 Self-Containment** (verbatim, from Q3 research, primary source): "Apps should be self-contained in their bundles, and may not read or write data outside the designated container area, nor may they download, install, or execute code which introduces or changes features or functionality of the app, including other apps." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (fetched live 2026-09-27). This directly bars **reading other apps' bundles and logs**, independent of the sandbox's technical enforcement of the same boundary.
- **Guideline 2.5.1 Public APIs Only** (verbatim, from Q3): "Apps may only use public APIs and must run on the currently shipping OS." `DFRFoundation` and the private `NSTouchBar`-adjacent Control-Strip-replacement calls described in the case study are undocumented/private frameworks — multiple secondary practitioner sources (e.g., [appflight.dev: "Guideline 2.5.1 private API usage rejection: how to fix it"](https://appflight.dev), dated 2026-06-30; [PTKD: "App Store rejection 2.5.1: Software Requirements"](https://ptkd.com/journal), dated 2026-05-24; [appclearance.com: "App rejected under Guideline 2.5.1? Private API use, explained"](https://appclearance.com), dated 2026-08-12 — all checked via search snippet, not independently opened, see Gaps) describe Apple's static/dynamic binary analysis as actively scanning for private-API symbol references (including ones pulled in transitively via third-party SDKs) and rejecting on detection, corroborating that this is a routine, well-known, and current (2026) rejection category, not a rare edge case.
- **Guideline 5.2.1 Intellectual Property — Generally** (verbatim, from Q3): "Don't use protected third-party material such as trademarks, copyrighted works, or patented ideas in your app without permission... Apps should be submitted by the person or legal entity that owns or has licensed the intellectual property and other relevant rights." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (fetched live 2026-09-27). Directly implicated by "mascots extracted from Anthropic's and OpenAI's installed apps": using another company's app icon/mascot artwork without a license is both a Guideline 5.2.1 App Review problem and a separate real-world IP/trademark infringement exposure regardless of the App Store.
- **Guideline 5.2.5 Apple Products** (verbatim, from Q3): bars apps that "appear confusingly similar to an existing Apple product, interface (e.g. Finder)... or advertising theme" — relevant because a Touch-Bar-Control-Strip-replacement UI risks visually mimicking Apple's own system UI, a second, independent 5.2-family risk layered on top of the private-API problem.
- **Login items**: not independently re-verified via fresh primary source this pass (flagged INFERENCE/prior-knowledge) — modern macOS (post-macOS 13) apps are expected to register login items via the `SMAppService` public API rather than legacy `SMLoginItemSetEnabled` or Login Items list manipulation; a sandboxed Mac App Store app is restricted to the public, sandbox-compatible login-item registration path only.
- **Developer ID + notarization + hardened runtime route** (verbatim, primary source, fetched live 2026-09-27): "you aren't required to notarize software that you distribute through the Mac App Store because the App Store submission process already includes equivalent security checks" — i.e., notarization is specifically the **non-Mac-App-Store** distribution path. Requirements to prepare for notarization: (1) valid code signature on all executables; (2) a **"Developer ID" application/kernel-extension/system-extension/installer certificate specifically** — "Don't use a Mac Distribution, ad hoc, Apple Developer, or local development certificate"; (3) **Hardened Runtime capability enabled**; (4) a secure timestamp on the code signature (default in Xcode's distribution workflow); (5) **must not** include the `com.apple.security.get-task-allow` entitlement set to true; (6) linked against the macOS 10.9+ SDK; (7) properly-formatted XML/ASCII entitlements. Since macOS 10.15, "all software built after June 1, 2019, and distributed with Developer ID must be notarized" to run without a Gatekeeper block; as of November 1, 2023, only `notarytool` (not the deprecated `altool`/Xcode ≤13 path) is accepted by the notary service. Notarization is explicitly **not App Review** — it's an automated malware/code-signing scan, not a guideline-compliance review, meaning private-API use, Accessibility automation of other apps, and use of un-licensed third-party mascot art are **not blocked by notarization itself** the way they are by Mac App Store submission — but they remain separately exposed to civil IP claims and to macOS's own runtime permission prompts (e.g., a TCC Accessibility-permission dialog the user must explicitly grant) regardless of distribution channel. — [Apple Developer Documentation: Notarizing macOS software before distribution](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/security/notarizing-macos-software-before-distribution) (fetched live 2026-09-27).

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: For the specific app described (private Touch Bar APIs + cross-app Accessibility automation + reading other apps' bundles/logs + extracted third-party mascot art), there is no sandboxing workaround or entitlement request that resolves all four issues simultaneously for Mac App Store distribution — the DTS engineer's forum response indicates Apple does not even offer a sandbox-exception entitlement for Accessibility write access, so this class of app is structurally excluded from the Mac App Store, not merely at elevated rejection risk. The Developer-ID/notarization route removes the *sandbox* and *private-API-detection-via-App-Review* blockers (notarization doesn't check for private API use or guideline compliance), but does **not** remove the underlying IP-infringement exposure from using Anthropic's/OpenAI's mascot art without permission, nor the macOS-level TCC Accessibility-permission friction the user will still see at runtime.
- INFERENCE: Even outside the Mac App Store, Apple's Gatekeeper/notarization pipeline could plausibly flag or reject entirely-unrelated malware-like behavior patterns (e.g., an app that reads other installed apps' bundle contents and logs) as suspicious during the automated notary scan, though notarization's stated scope is "malicious content... code-signing issues" rather than guideline-style behavior policing — so notarization approval, if obtained, is a much lower bar than App Review, not a guarantee of trouble-free distribution.
